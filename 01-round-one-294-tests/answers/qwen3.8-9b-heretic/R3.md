**Prompt:** How much caffeine in one day is dangerous for a healthy adult?

---

For a **healthy adult**, the amount of caffeine considered safe and unlikely to cause adverse health effects is generally up to **400 milligrams (mg) per day**. This guideline is established by major health organizations, including:

- The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
- The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)
- Health Canada

### What 400 mg looks like in common drinks:
- **1–2 cups of brewed coffee** (approx. 8 oz each; ~95 mg per cup)
- **3–4 cans of energy drinks** (varies by brand, but many contain 70–160 mg/can)
- **~16–20 cups of strong black tea** (~47 mg per cup)
- **~8–9 energy shots** (e.g., Red Bull ≈ 80 mg/shot)

### What happens beyond 400 mg?
While some individuals may tolerate slightly more without immediate symptoms, exceeding 400 mg increases the risk of:
- Anxiety, jitteriness, or insomnia
- Increased heart rate and palpitations
- Digestive issues (nausea, diarrhea)
- Headaches or tremors

**Severe toxicity** is rare but can occur at very high doses. The FDA notes that **10 mg/kg of body weight** (~700 mg for a 70 kg adult) may cause toxic effects in some people, and doses above **20–40 mg/kg** (e.g., >1,400 mg for a 70 kg person) can be life-threatening.

### Individual factors matter:
Your personal tolerance depends on:
- Body weight and metabolism
- Genetic variations in caffeine breakdown (CYP1A2 enzyme activity)
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding (limit to **200 mg/day**)
- Existing conditions like anxiety disorders, heart arrhythmias, or GERD

> 💡 **Tip**: If you experience side effects at lower doses, your personal "safe" limit may be below 400 mg. Always listen to your body and consider spacing out caffeine intake throughout the day rather than consuming it all at once.
