# qwen3.8-9b-heretic / M2

**Prompt:** 3 workers paint 3 walls in 3 hours. How long do 9 workers take to paint 9 walls?

---

### Step-by-Step Deduction

1.  **Analyze the rate of work for a single worker:**
    *   Given: 3 workers paint 3 walls in 3 hours.
    *   First, determine how long it takes one worker to paint one wall.
    *   If 3 workers take 3 hours to paint 3 walls, then collectively they produce 1 wall per hour (3 walls / 3 hours = 1 wall/hour total).
    *   Since there are 3 workers sharing that load: $1 \text{ wall/hour} \div 3 \text{ workers} = \frac{1}{3}$ of a wall per hour per worker.
    *   Alternatively, look at the individual perspective: One worker paints $\frac{1}{3}$ of a wall in 3 hours. Therefore, one worker takes $3 \times 3 = 9$ hours to paint **one** wall alone.

2.  **Calculate the time for the new scenario:**
    *   New task: Paint 9 walls.
    *   New workforce: 9 workers.
    *   Since each worker takes 9 hours to paint one wall, and we have 9 workers working simultaneously on 9 walls (one wall per worker), they can all work in parallel without waiting for others.
    *   The time required is simply the time it takes one worker to finish their assigned single wall.

3.  **Verification:**
    *   Rate of one worker = $\frac{1}{9}$ wall per hour.
    *   Total walls needed = 9.
    *   Number of workers = 9.
    *   Total rate = $9 \times \frac{1}{9} = 1$ wall per hour (collective).
    *   Time = $\frac{\text{Total Walls}}{\text{Collective Rate}} = \frac{9}{1} = 9$ hours.

### Final Conclusion

**9 hours.**
