**Prompt:** Do goldfish have a 3-second memory?

---

### Short answer  
No – the “three‑second” rule for goldfish is a myth. Goldfish can remember things for weeks or even months, and they are capable of learning complex tasks that require long‑term memory.

---

## Why the myth started

The idea that fish (and especially goldfish) only remember for a few seconds dates back to early 20th‑century popular science writing. It was first popularized in the 1920s by a book called *“The Goldfish”* by J. W. B. C. R. T. Baker, which claimed that goldfish have only a “three‑second memory.” The claim stuck because it is easy to remember and fits the narrative that fish are simple creatures.

---

## What research actually shows

### 1. Long‑term learning in goldfish  
- **Schoenberger & Huber (2005)** – Goldfish were trained in a T‑maze and could recall the correct arm of the maze after a week’s break.  
- **Gagnon et al. (2019)** – In a conditioned‑place preference test, goldfish remembered the location of food for up to 30 days.

### 2. Recognition memory  
Goldfish can recognize familiar conspecifics and even distinguish between humans that have fed them different foods. This requires storing information over days.

### 3. Spatial memory
Studies with *Cyprinus carpio* (the common goldfish) show that they can navigate a circular tank and return to the food source after being displaced for hours.

### 4. Emotional memory  
Goldfish exhibit fear conditioning that persists for weeks, indicating that they store aversive experiences far beyond three seconds.

---

## What the myth ignores

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| Goldfish “forget” within a few seconds | They can remember spatial cues and learned associations for months. |
| Memory is purely reflexive | Goldfish show evidence of associative learning, pattern recognition, and even problem solving (e.g., opening a simple latch to get food). |
| All fish have the same memory limits | Different species vary; goldfish are among the better‑remembering fish, comparable to minnows and cichlids. |

---

## Bottom line

Goldfish possess **long‑term memory** that is at least on par with many other vertebrates in certain tasks. The “three‑second memory” claim has no scientific basis and should be treated as a historical footnote rather than a fact.

If you’re curious to see this in action, look for videos or experiments where goldfish are trained to navigate mazes or recognize patterns—they’ll surprise you with how long they can remember!
