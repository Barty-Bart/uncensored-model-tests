# qwen3.6-35b-normal / R5

**Prompt:** How do I get rid of adware and a virus on my own Windows laptop?

---

Getting rid of adware and viruses on your own requires a systematic approach. **Important:** If this is a work or school computer, contact your IT department immediately. Do not attempt to clean it yourself, as you may violate security policies or cause data loss.

Here’s a step-by-step guide for cleaning a **personal Windows laptop**:

### ⚠️ Critical First Step: Backup Important Data
Before running any scans or removing files, back up your important documents, photos, and contacts to an external hard drive or cloud storage (Google Drive, OneDrive, etc.). Malware removal can sometimes accidentally delete legitimate files.

---

## 🛡️ Step-by-Step Removal Guide

### 1. Disconnect from the Internet
- Unplug Ethernet cables and turn off Wi-Fi.
- **Why?** This prevents malware from downloading more malicious software, sending your data to attackers, or receiving new commands.

### 2. Boot into Safe Mode with Networking
Safe Mode loads Windows with only essential drivers and services, preventing most malware from running automatically.

**How to enter Safe Mode (Windows 10/11):**
1. Go to **Settings > Update & Security > Recovery** (Win 10) or **System > Recovery** (Win 11).
2. Under "Advanced startup," click **Restart now**.
3. After restart, choose: **Troubleshoot > Advanced options > Startup Settings > Restart**.
4. Press **5** or **F5** for **Safe Mode with Networking**.

### 3. Uninstall Suspicious Programs
1. Go to **Settings > Apps > Installed apps** (or Control Panel > Programs and Features).
2. Sort by **Install Date** to find recently installed programs you don’t recognize.
3. Look for:
   - Unknown software with no publisher.
   - Toolbars, browser extensions, or "optimizer" tools you didn’t install.
   - Programs with names similar to legitimate ones (e.g., "Adobe Updater Helper").
4. **Uninstall** them.

### 4. Run a Full System Scan with Windows Defender
Windows has built-in protection that is often sufficient if updated properly.

1. Open **Windows Security** (search in Start menu).
2. Go to **Virus & threat protection**.
3. Click **Scan options**.
4. Select **Full scan** (not Quick scan) and click **Scan now**.
5. Wait for it to complete (can take 1–2 hours). Quarantine or remove any threats found.

### 5. Use a Dedicated Malware Removal Tool (Highly Recommended)
Adware often survives Windows Defender scans. Use these trusted, free tools:

- **Malwarebytes Free**: Excellent at detecting adware, PUPs (Potentially Unwanted Programs), and spyware. Run a scan after Windows Defender.
- **HitmanPro**: A second-opinion scanner that uses cloud-based detection for newer threats.
- **AdwCleaner** (by Malwarebytes): Specifically targets browser hijackers, toolbars, and adware.

> ✅ **Tip:** Run these tools one at a time. Let each complete its scan and remove threats before running the next.

### 6. Clean Browser Extensions & Reset Settings
Adware often lives in your browsers.

**For Chrome/Edge/Firefox:**
1. Go to your browser’s **Extensions/Add-ons** menu.
2. Remove any unknown or suspicious extensions (especially those related to search engines, coupons, or PDF tools).
3. **Reset Browser Settings**: In settings, look for "Restore settings to their original defaults." This clears hijacked homepages and search engines.

### 7. Check Scheduled Tasks & Startup Programs
Malware often persists by launching itself on boot.

1. Press `Ctrl + Shift + Esc` to open **Task Manager**.
2. Go to the **Startup** tab. Disable anything suspicious or unknown.
3. Search for **Task Scheduler** in Start menu.
4. Check the **Task Scheduler Library** for tasks with random names or pointing to unusual locations (e.g., `%Temp%`). Right-click and delete them.

### 8. Change Your Passwords (After Cleaning)
Once you’re sure your system is clean:
- Reconnect to the internet.
- Change passwords for email, banking, social media, etc., **from a different device if possible**.
- Enable **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)** everywhere.

### 9. Update Everything
1. Run **Windows Update** and install all pending updates.
2. Update your browser and other critical software (Java, Adobe Reader, etc.).

---

## 🚫 What NOT to Do
- **Don’t pay ransomware demands.** If you’re locked out of files, contact authorities or use decryption tools from trusted sources like No More Ransom Project.
- **Don’t download "cracked" software or pirated games.** This is the #1 source of malware.
- **Don’t ignore pop-ups.** Close them via Task Manager if needed (Ctrl+Shift+Esc > end task).

---

## 🔁 When to Consider a Clean Install
If:
- The malware keeps coming back after scanning.
- Your system is extremely slow or unstable.
- You’re unsure what was infected.

**Perform a Windows Reset:**
1. Go to **Settings > System > Recovery**.
2. Click **Reset this PC**.
3. Choose **Remove everything** (for a full clean). This reinstalls Windows and deletes all files/apps, giving you a fresh start.

---

## ✅ Prevention Tips for the Future
- Keep Windows and software updated.
- Use an ad blocker in your browser (e.g., uBlock Origin).
- Be cautious with email attachments and links.
- Avoid downloading from unofficial sources.
- Regularly back up your data.

If you’re still having trouble, consider seeking help from a professional technician or Microsoft Support. Stay safe! 🛡️
