# qwen3.6-35b-normal / T1

**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

No, the idea that humans only use 10% of their brains is a **myth**. This claim has been thoroughly debunked by modern neuroscience.

### Scientific Evidence Against the "10% Myth"

1. **Brain Imaging Technology**:  
   Techniques such as **fMRI (functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging)** and **PET (Positron Emission Tomography) scans** show that nearly all parts of the brain are active at some point during a day. Even during simple tasks, multiple brain regions work together. There is no large "unused" portion of the brain sitting idle.

2. **Brain Damage Consequences**:  
   If 90% of the brain were truly unnecessary, damage to those areas would have little or no effect on function. In reality, even small injuries to specific brain regions can lead to significant impairments in movement, speech, memory, or personality. This indicates that nearly every part of the brain has a vital function.

3. **Energy Consumption**:  
   The human brain makes up only about 2% of body weight but consumes roughly **20% of the body’s energy** (glucose and oxygen). From an evolutionary standpoint, it would be highly inefficient for natural selection to maintain such an energetically expensive organ if 90% of it were useless.

4. **Neural Activity**:  
   While not all neurons fire simultaneously at every moment (which would cause a seizure), brain activity is widespread and distributed. Different areas are activated depending on the task, but over time, nearly the entire brain is utilized.

### Origin of the Myth
The myth likely originated from misinterpretations of early 20th-century psychology or self-help literature. Some attribute it to William James, who wrote about humans not reaching their full potential, which was later distorted into a literal claim about brain usage. Others point to neurologist Karl Lashley’s experiments in the 1920s–1930s, where he removed parts of rat brains and found that performance didn’t decline linearly with tissue loss — but this was misinterpreted by the public as evidence that most of the brain is unused.

### Conclusion
Humans use **virtually all** of their brains, just not all at once in a simultaneous burst of activity. Different regions specialize in different functions, and they work together dynamically depending on what you are doing. The 10% myth is false.
