# qwen3.6-35b-normal / M2

**Prompt:** 3 workers paint 3 walls in 3 hours. How long do 9 workers take to paint 9 walls?

---

To solve this problem, we need to determine the rate at which a single worker paints and then apply that rate to the new scenario.

### Step 1: Determine the work rate of one worker

First, let's analyze the initial situation:
*   **3 workers** paint **3 walls** in **3 hours**.

Since all workers are working simultaneously and independently, we can look at what a single worker accomplishes in that time frame. If 3 workers together finish 3 walls in 3 hours, it implies that each individual worker is responsible for painting exactly one of those walls.

Therefore:
*   **1 worker** paints **1 wall** in **3 hours**.

The rate of work is:
$$ \text{Rate} = \frac{1 \text{ wall}}{3 \text{ hours}} $$

### Step 2: Apply the rate to the new scenario

Now, consider the new situation:
*   We have **9 workers**.
*   They need to paint **9 walls**.

Since we established that **1 worker** takes **3 hours** to paint **1 wall**, we can scale this up linearly because the number of workers and the number of walls increase by the same factor (a factor of 3).

If you have 9 workers, each worker can take on one wall.
*   Worker 1 paints Wall 1.
*   Worker 2 paints Wall 2.
*   ...
*   Worker 9 paints Wall 9.

Since they are all working simultaneously, the time taken is determined by how long it takes a single worker to paint their single assigned wall.

### Calculation

$$ \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Work per worker}}{\text{Rate per worker}} $$
$$ \text{Time} = \frac{1 \text{ wall}}{1/3 \text{ walls/hour}} = 3 \text{ hours} $$

Alternatively, using the proportionality method:
$$ \frac{W_1}{N_1 \times T_1} = \frac{W_2}{N_2 \times T_2} $$
Where $W$ is walls, $N$ is workers, and $T$ is time.

$$ \frac{3}{3 \times 3} = \frac{9}{9 \times T_2} $$
$$ \frac{3}{9} = \frac{9}{9 T_2} $$
$$ \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{T_2} $$
$$ T_2 = 3 $$

### Conclusion

It will take **3 hours** for 9 workers to paint 9 walls.
