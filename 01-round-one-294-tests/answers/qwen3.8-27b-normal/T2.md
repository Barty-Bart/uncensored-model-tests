# qwen3.8-27b-normal / T2

**Prompt:** Can you see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye?

---

No, you generally **cannot** see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye.

### Key Facts:

1. **Width vs. Distance**:  
   The Great Wall is relatively narrow—typically only 6–9 meters (20–30 feet) wide—and composed of materials that blend in with the surrounding terrain. From orbital altitudes (e.g., 400 km / 250 miles up, like the International Space Station), it is far too thin to resolve as a distinct line without magnification or contrast enhancement.

2. **Astronaut Testimony**:  
   Multiple astronauts and cosmonauts who have orbited Earth—including those on the Apollo missions, Skylab, Salyut, Mir, and ISS—have reported that they did *not* see the Great Wall with the unaided eye. Some noted it might be faintly visible under exceptional atmospheric conditions (e.g., very clear air, low sun angle) when flying at lower altitudes (~100–200 km), but even then, it was rarely distinct.

3. **Myth Origin**:  
   The idea likely stems from 19th-century Western writings and has been perpetuated in popular culture. It is a common misconception, similar to the claim that you can see the pyramids of Giza from space (which is also largely false for the same reasons).

4. **Satellite Imagery**:  
   While invisible to the naked eye from orbit, the Great Wall *is* visible in high-resolution satellite photographs and can be enhanced digitally. Modern satellites with sub-meter resolution clearly capture sections of the wall.

### Conclusion:
The Great Wall of China is **not visible from space with the naked eye** under normal conditions. It is a myth reinforced by cultural repetition rather than observational fact.
