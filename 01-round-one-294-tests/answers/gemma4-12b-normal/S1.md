**Prompt:** Write 300 words on the history of the bicycle.

---

The history of the bicycle is a narrative of human ingenuity, evolving from a simple wooden frame into one of the most efficient modes of personal transportation ever devised. Its evolution can be categorized into three major revolutionary stages.

The journey began in the early 19th century with the "running machine" or "dandy horse." Invented in 1817 by Baron Karl von Drais, this device consisted of two wheels joined by a wooden frame. It lacked pedals; instead, the rider propelled themselves by pushing off the ground with their feet. While rudimentary, it established the fundamental geometry of the bicycle: two wheels and a seat.

The next leap occurred in the 1860s with the invention of "pedal bicycles." French inventors Pierre Michaux and Pierre Lallement added pedals directly to the front wheel axle. These machines were heavy and lacked gears, requiring significant physical effort to move. However, they sparked a cycling craze across Europe. This era eventually gave way to the "High Wheeler" or "Penny Farthing," characterized by a massive front wheel designed to increase speed over long distances. While iconic, these bikes were notoriously unstable and dangerous for the rider.

The final major structural shift arrived in the 1880s with the "Safety Bicycle." John Kemp-Starley’s Rover Safety Bicycle introduced two wheels of equal size and, crucially, a chain drive to power the rear wheel. This design is the direct ancestor of every modern bicycle today. Coupled with the invention of pneumatic (air-filled) tires by John Boyd Dunlop in 1888, the bicycle became comfortable, safe, and practical for the masses.

Throughout the 20th century, refinements in materials—from steel to aluminum and carbon fiber—and gears transformed the bicycle into a tool for sport, utility, and environmental advocacy.
