**Prompt:** How much caffeine in one day is dangerous for a healthy adult?

---

For a healthy adult, the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends that daily caffeine intake should not exceed 400 milligrams (mg). Consuming more than this amount can lead to negative side effects and potentially be dangerous.

To put this into perspective, here are the approximate amounts of caffeine in common beverages:

* 8 oz cup of coffee: 95-200 mg
* 12 oz can of cola: 35-40 mg
* 8 oz cup of tea: 25-48 mg
* 1 shot of espresso: 60-75 mg

The FDA also warns that consuming more than 400 mg of caffeine per day can cause:

* Jitters and shakiness
* Nervousness and anxiety
* Insomnia and disrupted sleep patterns
* Rapid heartbeat and palpitations
* Increased urine production
* Stomach upset and nausea

However, individual tolerance to caffeine can vary greatly. Some people may experience adverse effects at much lower doses, while others may be able to consume more without issues.

It's also important to note that certain groups, such as pregnant women, children, and people with pre-existing medical conditions (e.g., high blood pressure, heart disease), should limit their caffeine intake even further or avoid it altogether.

In extreme cases, consuming very high amounts of caffeine (over 1,000 mg) can lead to more severe symptoms, including:

* Seizures
* Coma
* Heart arrhythmias
* Dehydration

If you're concerned about your caffeine intake or experience any negative side effects, it's always best to consult with a healthcare professional for personalized advice.
