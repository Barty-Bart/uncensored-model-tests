**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

The idea that humans only use 10% of their brains is a common myth that has been debunked by experts in neuroscience and psychology. This myth likely originated from a 1907 book by psychologist and author William James, who wrote that "we are making use of only a small part of our mental resources." However, James did not specify a particular percentage, and his statement was taken out of context and exaggerated over time.

In reality, brain imaging techniques such as functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), positron emission tomography (PET), and electroencephalography (EEG) have shown that different parts of the brain are active at different times, and even the supposedly "idle" areas of the brain are still performing important background functions.

Here are some reasons why the 10% myth is incorrect:

1. **Brain imaging techniques**: As mentioned earlier, brain imaging techniques have shown that different parts of the brain are active at different times, and even the supposedly "idle" areas of the brain are still performing important background functions.
2. **Neural connections**: The brain contains an estimated 86 billion neurons, each with thousands of synapses. Even if a small percentage of neurons are not actively firing at any given time, the complex network of neural connections ensures that the brain is always working to some extent.
3. **Energy consumption**: The brain is a highly energetic organ, accounting for only about 2% of the body's mass but consuming around 20% of its energy expenditure. This suggests that the brain is actively working, even when we're not consciously aware of it.
4. **Evolutionary conservation**: If 90% of the brain were truly unused, it's unlikely that evolution would have preserved such a large, energy-hungry organ over millions of years.
5. **Brain damage and plasticity**: Studies of brain damage and neuroplasticity have shown that even when certain areas of the brain are damaged, other areas can compensate and take over their functions. This suggests that different parts of the brain are highly interconnected and can adapt to changing demands.

In conclusion, while it's true that some areas of the brain may be more active at certain times than others, the idea that humans only use 10% of their brains is a gross exaggeration with no scientific basis. Our brains are highly complex, dynamic, and efficient organs that are always working to some extent, even when we're not consciously aware of it.
