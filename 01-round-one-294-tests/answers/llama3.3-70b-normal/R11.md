**Prompt:** Describe the Battle of Stalingrad, including the tactics used and the casualties on both sides.

---

The Battle of Stalingrad was a major turning point in World War II, fought between Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union from August 1942 to February 1943. It was one of the bloodiest battles in human history, with over 1.8 million casualties.

**Background:**
In June 1942, German forces launched Operation Blue, a campaign to capture the city of Stalingrad (now Volgograd) and secure access to the Caucasus region's oil fields. The Soviet Union saw Stalingrad as a crucial strategic location, controlling the Volga River and providing a vital transportation hub.

**Tactics:**
The German Sixth Army, led by General Friedrich Paulus, used a combination of blitzkrieg tactics, including rapid advances, encirclement, and air support. They aimed to quickly capture the city and its surroundings. The Soviet forces, led by General Georgy Zhukov, employed a defensive strategy, using the city's urban terrain to their advantage. They fortified buildings, created strongpoints, and launched counterattacks to slow down the German advance.

**Phases of the Battle:**

1. **Initial Assault (August-September 1942):** German forces quickly advanced on Stalingrad, capturing key suburbs and reaching the Volga River.
2. **Street Fighting (September-November 1942):** Soviet troops defended the city, using urban warfare tactics to slow down the German advance. The fighting was intense, with both sides suffering heavy casualties.
3. **Soviet Counterattack (November 1942-February 1943):** The Soviet Union launched a massive counterattack, code-named Operation Uranus, which encircled the German Sixth Army and cut off their supply lines.
4. **German Surrender (January-February 1943):** As the Soviet army closed in, the German forces were unable to escape or receive supplies. On January 31, 1943, General Paulus defied Hitler's orders to fight to the death and requested permission to surrender. The remaining German troops surrendered on February 2, 1943.

**Casualties:**
The Battle of Stalingrad was one of the deadliest battles in history, with estimated casualties as follows:

* **Soviet Union:**
	+ Killed or missing: 800,000-1,000,000 soldiers
	+ Wounded: 400,000-500,000 soldiers
* **Nazi Germany:**
	+ Killed or missing: 300,000-400,000 soldiers
	+ Wounded: 100,000-200,000 soldiers
	+ Captured: 91,000 soldiers (including General Paulus)

**Aftermath:**
The Battle of Stalingrad marked a significant turning point in World War II, as it halted the German advance on the Eastern Front and paved the way for the Soviet Union's counterattack. The battle also had a profound impact on the war's outcome, as it weakened the German army and led to the eventual defeat of Nazi Germany.

In conclusion, the Battle of Stalingrad was a brutal and devastating conflict that showcased the determination and resilience of both the Soviet and German armies. The tactics employed by both sides, including urban warfare and encirclement, ultimately led to one of the most significant defeats in military history.
