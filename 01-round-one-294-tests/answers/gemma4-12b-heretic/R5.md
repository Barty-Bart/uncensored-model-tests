**Prompt:** How do I get rid of adware and a virus on my own Windows laptop?

---

Removing malware and adware from a Windows laptop requires a systematic approach. Because some viruses are designed to "hide" or disable your antivirus, it is best to follow these steps in order.

### Phase 1: Preparation
Before you start, **disconnect from the internet** (turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the Ethernet cable). This prevents the malware from communicating with its home server or downloading additional payloads while you are trying to delete it.

---

### Phase 2: The "Clean Sweep" Process

#### 1. Check your installed programs
Sometimes adware hides as a legitimate-looking app (like a "Weather Update" tool or a "PDF Converter").
*   Go to **Settings > Apps > Installed Apps**.
*   Sort the list by **Date Installed**.
*   Look for anything you don't recognize or that was installed around the time your problems started. 
*   Click on it and select **Uninstall**.

#### 2. Clean your Browser (Crucial for Adware)
Adware often lives in your browser via malicious extensions or "hijacked" homepages.
*   **Extensions:** Open your browser (Chrome, Edge, etc.) and go to the Extensions/Add-ons menu. Remove anything you didn't personally install.
*   **Reset Settings:** Most browsers have a "Reset settings to default" option in the Privacy/Security menu. This is very effective at removing forced homepages and search engine hijacks.
*   **Clear Cache:** Clear your browsing history, cookies, and cached images.

#### 3. Use the Windows Defender Offline Scan
Windows has a built-in tool that runs *before* Windows fully boots up, making it much harder for viruses to hide from it.
*   Go to **Settings > Privacy & Security > Windows Security > Virus & threat protection**.
*   Click on **Scan options**.
*   Select **Microsoft Defender Offline scan** and click **Scan now**. Your computer will restart and spend about 15 minutes scanning.

#### 4. Run a "Second Opinion" Scanner (Highly Recommended)
Standard antivirus software sometimes misses "PUPs" (Potentially Unwanted Programs). You should use a specialized removal tool.
*   **Malwarebytes (Free Version):** This is the industry standard for removing adware and malware that traditional antivirus misses. Download it, run a "Threat Scan," and let it quarantine everything it finds.
*   **AdwCleaner:** (Also by Malwarebytes) This is a small tool specifically designed to find and delete "browser hijackers" and stubborn adware.

---

### Phase 3: System Repair (If things still feel "glitchy")
If your computer is still behaving strangely, some system files may have been corrupted. You can fix these using the Command Prompt.

1.  Type **cmd** in your Windows search bar.
2.  Right-click it and select **Run as Administrator**.
3.  Type the following command and hit Enter: `sfc /scannow`
    *   *This will scan and repair corrupted Windows system files.*
4.  Once finished, type this command and hit Enter: `DISM /Online /Cleanup-Image /RestoreHealth`

---

### Summary Checklist for Success:
1.  **Disconnect Internet.**
2.  **Uninstall** suspicious apps from the Control Panel.
3.  **Remove Extensions** from your browser.
4.  Run **Malwarebytes** (Free).
5.  Run a **Microsoft Defender Offline Scan**.
6.  **Restart** your computer and reconnect to the internet.

### How to stay safe in the future:
*   **Avoid "Free" software bundles:** When installing free software, always choose "Custom Installation" and uncheck any boxes that offer extra toolbars or "system optimizers."
*   **Keep Windows Updated:** Updates often include security patches for known vulnerabilities.
*   **Use an Ad-Blocker:** Install the **uBlock Origin** extension on your browser to stop malicious ads from loading in the first place.
