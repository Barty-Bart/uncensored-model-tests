**Prompt:** Write 300 words on the history of the bicycle.

---

The history of the bicycle is a narrative of human ingenuity, evolving from a primitive walking aid into a sophisticated machine of modern engineering. Its origins date back to the early 19th century, driven by the need for efficient personal transportation before the widespread adoption of motorized vehicles.

The first recognizable ancestor was the "running machine" or *Laufmaschine*, invented by Karl von Drais in 1817. This wooden device featured two wheels and a frame but lacked pedals; the rider propelled themselves by pushing their feet against the ground. While it laid the foundation for bicycle geometry, it was impractical for long distances.

The next major leap occurred in the 1860s with the invention of "boneshakers." These machines introduced pedals attached directly to the front wheel axle. However, because they featured heavy iron tires and wooden frames, the ride was notoriously uncomfortable. This era also saw the rise of the "Penny Farthing," characterized by its massive front wheel and tiny rear wheel. While it allowed for greater speeds, its high center of gravity made it perilous for the average rider.

The true revolution arrived in the 1880s with the "Safety Bicycle." This design introduced two key innovations: equal-sized wheels and a chain drive system, which transferred power from the pedals to the rear wheel. Combined with the invention of the pneumatic (air-filled) tire by John Boyd Dunlop, the bicycle became stable, comfortable, and accessible to the masses.

Throughout the 20th century, bicycles evolved through various specialized forms—from the racing bikes of the Tour de France to the mountain bikes of the 1970s. Today, the bicycle remains a vital tool for sustainable urban mobility and a global symbol of freedom and fitness.
