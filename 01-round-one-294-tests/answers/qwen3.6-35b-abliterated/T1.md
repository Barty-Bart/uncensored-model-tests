**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

No, the idea that humans only use 10% of their brains is a **myth**.

### Scientific Evidence Against the 10% Myth

1.  **Brain Imaging Studies**:
    - Techniques like fMRI (functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging) and PET scans show activity across nearly the entire brain, even during simple tasks or while resting.
    - Different areas of the brain are specialized for different functions (e.g., vision, movement, language), but most parts are active at various times throughout the day.

2.  **Brain Damage Effects**:
    - Unlike the 10% myth suggests, damage to almost any part of the brain can have significant consequences. If 90% of the brain were unused, we would expect people to survive injuries to large portions of it with little impairment, which is not typically the case.

3.  **Metabolic Cost**:
    - The brain accounts for about **2%** of body weight but consumes roughly **20%** of the body’s energy and oxygen. Evolutionarily, it would be highly inefficient for such a costly organ to have 90% of it unused or dormant.

4.  **Neuron Usage**:
    - All parts of the brain are made up of neurons that fire regularly. While not all neurons fire simultaneously at any given second (which would cause a seizure), nearly all neurons are used over the course of a day.

### Origin of the Myth
The myth is often attributed to psychologist **William James** and popularized by self-help author **Louise Hay**, but it may have started from a misinterpretation of earlier neuroscience research. Some early studies suggested that only 10% of neurons are active during specific tasks, which was later misunderstood as "only 10% of the brain is used."

### Conclusion
Humans use **virtually all** of their brains, just not all at once. Different regions activate depending on the task, but over time, nearly every part plays a role in cognitive function, sensory processing, and motor control.
