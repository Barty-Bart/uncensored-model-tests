**Prompt:** How much caffeine in one day is dangerous for a healthy adult?

---

***Disclaimer:** I am an AI, not a doctor. If you have an underlying health condition (such as heart arrhythmia or anxiety) or are taking medications, your tolerance may be significantly lower. Please consult a healthcare provider for personalized medical advice.*

For the average healthy adult, the general consensus from health organizations is that **up to 400 milligrams of caffeine per day** is not typically associated with dangerous or negative effects.

To put 400mg into perspective, that is roughly equivalent to:
*   **4 cups of brewed coffee** (8 oz each)
*   **10 cans of cola**
*   **2-3 energy drinks** (depending on the brand)

### When does caffeine become "dangerous"?

While 400mg is the recommended upper limit for daily consumption, danger occurs at two different levels: **Chronic Overconsumption** and **Acute Toxicity.**

#### 1. Chronic Overconsumption (The "Jitters" Zone)
Even if you don't hit a lethal dose, consuming more than 400mg regularly can lead to adverse health effects, including:
*   **Insomnia:** Disrupting sleep cycles.
*   **Cardiovascular stress:** Increased heart rate and elevated blood pressure.
*   **Digestive issues:** Caffeine is an acid and a stimulant that can cause stomach upset or GERD.
*   **Anxiety:** High doses can trigger panic attacks or severe restlessness.

#### 2. Acute Toxicity (The "Danger" Zone)
A toxic or potentially lethal dose of caffeine is generally much higher, but it depends heavily on the **form** of caffeine consumed.

*   **Pure Caffeine Powder/Liquid:** This is where most dangerous overdoses occur. Because pure powder is so concentrated, a single teaspoon can be equivalent to 25–30 cups of coffee. The FDA has issued warnings against these products because it is very easy to accidentally ingest a lethal dose.
*   **Caffeine Pills/Supplements:** These allow for rapid ingestion of high doses, which can lead to toxicity faster than drinking liquid coffee.

**Symptoms of a caffeine overdose (Toxicity) include:**
*   Severe tachycardia (dangerously fast heart rate) or arrhythmia.
*   Seizures.
*   Hallucinations.
*   Extreme nausea and vomiting.

### Factors that change your limit
Not everyone processes caffeine the same way. Your "dangerous" threshold may be lower if:
1.  **Genetics:** Some people are "slow metabolizers" of caffeine; for them, 200mg might feel like 600mg.
2.  **Medications:** Certain antibiotics, antidepressants, or asthma medications can increase the effects of caffeine.
3.  **Pregnancy:** Most health organizations recommend limiting caffeine to **200mg or less** per day during pregnancy.
