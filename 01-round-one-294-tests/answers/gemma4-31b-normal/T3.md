**Prompt:** Do goldfish have a 3-second memory?

---

The short answer is **no**. The idea that goldfish have a three-second memory is a widespread urban legend and is scientifically incorrect.

Research has shown that goldfish are actually quite intelligent for fish and possess memories that last for months, not seconds. Here is the evidence:

### 1. Learning and Training
Scientists have successfully trained goldfish to perform complex tasks. For example, researchers have taught goldfish to push a lever to receive food at specific times of the day. The fish remembered the timing and the action long after the initial training period ended.

### 2. Spatial Memory (Mazes)
Goldfish are capable of navigating mazes. Once they learn the path to a reward, they can remember that route for several weeks. If they had a three-second memory, they would have to "re-learn" the maze every time they turned a corner.

### 3. Social Recognition
Goldfish can recognize their owners and distinguish between different people. They often react differently to the person who feeds them than they do to a stranger, which requires long-term memory of faces and behaviors.

### 4. Auditory Cues
Studies have shown that goldfish can be trained to respond to specific sounds (like a bell or a buzzer) that signal feeding time. This association remains stable over long periods.

### Where did the myth come from?
It is unclear exactly where the "three-second" figure originated, but it likely persists because it makes people feel better about keeping fish in small, boring bowls. If a fish forgot everything every three seconds, a tiny bowl wouldn't be "boring" to them. In reality, because goldfish are intelligent and curious, they require plenty of space and mental stimulation to thrive.
