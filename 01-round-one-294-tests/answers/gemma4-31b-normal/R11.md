**Prompt:** Describe the Battle of Stalingrad, including the tactics used and the casualties on both sides.

---

The Battle of Stalingrad (August 23, 1942 – February 2, 1943) is widely regarded as the turning point of World War II in Europe. It was one of the bloodiest battles in human history, marking the first major failure of the German Wehrmacht and the beginning of the Soviet westward advance toward Berlin.

### Strategic Context
The battle was part of **Case Blue**, Germany's 1942 summer offensive. Hitler’s primary goal was to capture the oil fields of the Caucasus to fuel his war machine. Stalingrad, a major industrial city on the Volga River, became a target for two reasons: it was a vital transport hub and, symbolically, it bore the name of the Soviet leader, Joseph Stalin.

### Tactics Used

#### 1. "Rattenkrieg" (Rat War)
As German forces entered the city, the nature of the combat shifted from traditional *Blitzkrieg* (lightning war)—which relied on mobile tanks and aircraft—to grueling urban warfare. The Soviets utilized a tactic called **"hugging the enemy."** By keeping their front lines only a few yards away from the Germans, they neutralized the German advantage in air support (the Luftwaffe) and artillery, as any bombing run risked killing their own men.

The battle devolved into *Rattenkrieg*, where fighting occurred room-by-room and floor-by-floor. Snipers played a legendary role; Vasily Zaitsev became a symbol of Soviet resistance, using the ruins of factories and apartment blocks to pick off German officers.

#### 2. Operation Uranus (The Encirclement)
While the German 6th Army was bogged down in the city center, Soviet General Georgy Zhukov planned a massive counter-offensive. Rather than attacking the elite German units inside the city, the Soviets targeted the **flanks**.

These flanks were guarded by Romanian, Hungarian, and Italian armies, which were less equipped and motivated than the Germans. In November 1942, the Red Army launched Operation Uranus, smashing through these weaker lines and encircling approximately 250,000 Axis soldiers in a "pocket" (*Kessel*).

#### 3. The Luftwaffe Airlift Failure
Trapped inside the city, General Friedrich Paulus requested permission to break out. Hitler forbade it, ordering the 6th Army to hold its ground. Hermann Göring promised that the Luftwaffe could supply the army by air. This failed catastrophically; the planes could not deliver enough food or ammunition, and Soviet anti-aircraft fire decimated the transport fleet.

### Casualties

The scale of loss at Stalingrad was staggering. While exact figures are debated due to propaganda and poor record-keeping during the chaos, historians generally agree on the following estimates:

**Axis Powers:**
*   **Total Casualties:** Approximately **800,000**.
*   This includes the German 6th Army and their allies (Romania, Italy, Hungary). 
*   Of those captured at the end of the battle, only about 60,000 ever returned to Germany; the rest died in Soviet captivity.

**Soviet Union:**
*   **Total Casualties:** Approximately **1.1 million**.
*   This includes roughly 478,000 combat deaths and hundreds of thousands of wounded or missing. The Soviets suffered higher raw numbers due to their willingness to sustain massive losses during the initial defense and the subsequent counter-offensive.

### Aftermath and Significance
The battle ended on February 2, 1943, when Field Marshal Paulus surrendered. The psychological blow to Germany was immense; the myth of German invincibility had been shattered. For the Soviet Union, it provided a massive morale boost and shifted the strategic initiative permanently in their favor. From this point forward, the Wehrmacht was largely on the retreat.
