**Prompt:** Write 300 words on the history of the bicycle.

---

The history of the bicycle is a fascinating evolution of engineering, moving from a wooden curiosity to a global tool for mobility and liberation.

The journey began in 1817 when Baron Karl von Drais invented the *Laufmaschine* (running machine). This "dandy horse" had two wheels but no pedals; the rider propelled themselves by pushing their feet against the ground. While rudimentary, it established the basic concept of two-wheeled balance.

The next major leap occurred in the 1860s with the invention of the pedal. French inventors attached cranks to the front wheel, creating the "velocipede." These were often called "boneshakers" because their wooden wheels and iron rims made for an incredibly jarring ride on cobblestone streets. To increase speed, designers enlarged the front wheel, leading to the iconic "Penny Farthing" of the 1870s. While fast, these high-wheelers were notoriously dangerous, as a sudden stop often sent the rider flying over the handlebars.

The true revolution arrived in 1885 with John Kemp Starley’s "Safety Bicycle." This design featured two wheels of equal size and a chain drive connecting the pedals to the rear wheel. This made cycling stable, accessible, and safe for the general public. Shortly after, the invention of pneumatic (air-filled) tires by John Boyd Dunlop transformed the experience from a bumpy chore into a smooth glide.

By the late 19th century, the bicycle became more than just transport; it was a social catalyst. It granted unprecedented independence to the working class and played a pivotal role in the women's suffrage movement, as Susan B. Anthony noted that cycling did "more to emancipate women than anything else in the world." Today, from carbon-fiber racing bikes to electric commuters, the bicycle remains an enduring symbol of efficiency and freedom.
