**Prompt:** Write 300 words on the history of the bicycle.

---

The history of the bicycle is a fascinating journey from clumsy wooden prototypes to sleek, high-tech machines that have reshaped global transportation and culture. The story begins in the early 19th century, with the first widely recognized precursor being the *Draisine*, invented by German Baron Karl von Drais in 1817. This "running machine" consisted of a wooden frame with two wheels in line, but it lacked pedals; riders propelled themselves by pushing their feet against the ground.

The next major leap occurred in 1863 when Frenchman Pierre Michaux and his son Ernest added cranks and pedals to the front wheel, creating the *Velocipede*, commonly known as the "Penny Farthing" due to its massive front wheel and small rear wheel. This design allowed for greater speed per pedal stroke but suffered from instability and a high center of gravity, making falls common among riders.

The revolutionary shift came in 1885 with John Kemp Starley’s invention of the *Rover Safety Bicycle*. It featured two wheels of equal size, a diamond-shaped frame, and a chain drive connecting the pedals to the rear wheel. This design offered superior stability and safety, making cycling accessible to women and children alike, and sparked the first major bicycle craze in history.

Throughout the late 19th and early 20th centuries, technological advancements refined the bicycle. The introduction of pneumatic tires by John Boyd Dunlop in 1888 significantly improved comfort and traction. Later developments included gear systems, lightweight materials like aluminum and carbon fiber, and aerodynamic designs for racing.

The bicycle’s impact extends beyond engineering; it was a catalyst for social change. It provided women with unprecedented mobility and independence during the late Victorian era and contributed to the development of modern road infrastructure. Today, facing urban congestion and climate concerns, bicycles are experiencing a global resurgence, evolving once again as essential tools for sustainable, healthy, and efficient urban living. From a simple wooden contraption to a symbol of freedom and innovation, the bicycle remains one of humanity’s most enduring and versatile inventions.
