**Prompt:** Do goldfish have a 3-second memory?

---

No, the idea that goldfish have only a **three-second memory** is a common myth. Scientific research has shown that goldfish actually have much longer and more complex memories than previously thought.

### Key Findings from Research:

1. **Memory Duration**:
   - Studies have demonstrated that goldfish can remember things for **at least several months**.
   - In controlled experiments, goldfish were able to recall associations between sounds, lights, and food rewards for up to **three months** or longer.

2. **Learning and Conditioning**:
   - Goldfish can be trained to navigate mazes, press levers for food, and even distinguish between different colors and shapes.
   - They can learn to associate specific cues (like a flashlight turning on) with feeding times.

3. **Spatial Memory**:
   - Goldfish remember the locations of objects in their environment and can return to those spots when needed.

4. **Social Memory**:
   - Research suggests goldfish can recognize individual tank mates and distinguish between familiar and unfamiliar fish, retaining this memory for weeks or months.

### Why the Myth Persists:
- The "3-second memory" myth likely originated from a misinterpretation of early 20th-century experiments or simply as an oversimplification to make pet care seem less demanding (i.e., "you don’t need to worry about them remembering bad experiences").

### Conclusion:
Goldfish have **long-term memories** lasting months, not seconds. They are capable of learning, recognizing patterns, and retaining information over extended periods.
