# qwen3.6-35b-heretic / T1

**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

No, the idea that humans only use 10% of their brains is a **myth**. This popular belief has been extensively debunked by modern neuroscience.

### Key Facts:

1. **Whole-Brain Usage**:  
   Neuroimaging technologies like fMRI (functional magnetic resonance imaging) and PET (positron emission tomography) scans show that we use virtually all parts of the brain, even during simple tasks or rest. Different areas are active at different times depending on the activity.

2. **No "Unused" Regions**:  
   While not every neuron fires simultaneously at once, most of the brain is active throughout the day. There are no large silent zones that remain completely unused under normal conditions.

3. **Brain Damage Effects**:  
   If 90% of the brain were truly unused, damage to those areas would have little effect. However, even small injuries to specific regions can cause significant functional impairments (e.g., speech, movement, memory), indicating that nearly all parts serve important functions.

4. **Energy Consumption**:  
   The brain makes up only about 2% of body weight but consumes ~20% of the body’s energy and oxygen. Evolutionarily, it would be highly inefficient to maintain such an energetically expensive organ if 90% were unnecessary.

5. **Origin of the Myth**:  
   The myth likely stems from a misinterpretation of early 20th-century neuroscience or self-help literature. One possible source is psychologist William James, who suggested humans only use a *fraction* of their *mental potential*, not physical brain tissue. It was popularized in the 1990s by movies like *Lucy* (2014), which exaggerated the concept for dramatic effect.

### Conclusion:
Humans use **nearly all** of their brains, just not all at once. Different regions specialize in different tasks, and they work together in complex networks to support cognition, emotion, movement, and sensory processing.
