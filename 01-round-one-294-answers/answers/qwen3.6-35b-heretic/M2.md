# qwen3.6-35b-heretic / M2

**Prompt:** 3 workers paint 3 walls in 3 hours. How long do 9 workers take to paint 9 walls?

---

To solve this problem, we need to determine the rate at which the workers are painting and then apply that to the new scenario.

### Step 1: Determine the individual work rate

First, let's analyze the initial situation:
*   **3 workers** paint **3 walls** in **3 hours**.

This implies that each worker is working independently on one wall. If we look at just one of those workers:
*   That single worker paints 1 wall in 3 hours.

Therefore, the rate of work for one worker is:
$$ \text{Rate} = \frac{1 \text{ wall}}{3 \text{ hours}} $$

### Step 2: Analyze the new scenario

Now we have:
*   **9 workers**
*   **9 walls** to paint

Since each worker has a rate of painting 1 wall every 3 hours, let's see what happens if all 9 workers work simultaneously on separate walls.

If Worker 1 paints Wall 1 in 3 hours, and Worker 2 paints Wall 2 in 3 hours, ..., and Worker 9 paints Wall 9 in 3 hours, then:
*   All 9 walls are being painted concurrently by 9 different workers.
*   Since each worker takes 3 hours to complete their single assigned wall, the total time for the group to finish all 9 walls is determined by the time it takes one worker to finish their task.

### Step 3: Verification using Work Formula

We can also use the formula:
$$ \text{Work} = \text{Rate} \times \text{Time} \times \text{Number of Workers} $$

From the first scenario:
$$ 3 \text{ walls} = R \times 3 \text{ hours} \times 3 \text{ workers} $$
$$ 3 = 9R \implies R = \frac{1}{3} \text{ wall per hour per worker} $$

For the second scenario:
*   Work = 9 walls
*   Workers = 9
*   Rate ($R$) = $\frac{1}{3}$

$$ 9 = \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \times T \times 9 $$
$$ 9 = 3T $$
$$ T = \frac{9}{3} $$
$$ T = 3 \text{ hours} $$

### Conclusion

It takes **3 hours** for 9 workers to paint 9 walls.
