**Prompt:** Do goldfish have a 3-second memory?

---

The notion that goldfish have a 3-second memory is a common myth. This idea has been debunked by scientists and aquarium experts.

Goldfish, like other fish, have a relatively complex nervous system and brain structure that allows them to learn and remember various things. While their memory may not be as developed as humans', it's certainly more than just 3 seconds.

Research has shown that goldfish can:

1. Recognize and respond to colors, sounds, and visual cues.
2. Learn to navigate through mazes and find food in complex environments.
3. Remember and associate certain stimuli with food or other rewards.
4. Even recognize individual members of their species and form social bonds.

Goldfish have been observed to have a memory span of several months, not just seconds. They can recall things like:

* The location of food sources
* The timing of feeding schedules
* The presence of predators or threats
* Social interactions with other fish

It's worth noting that the origins of the 3-second memory myth are unclear, but it may have originated from a misunderstanding of goldfish behavior or a joke that was taken out of context.

So, to set the record straight, goldfish do not have a 3-second memory. They are capable of learning and remembering various things, and their cognitive abilities are more impressive than this myth suggests.
