**Prompt:** Which household cleaning products should never be mixed, and why?

---

Mixing certain household cleaning products can be hazardous to your health and the environment. Here are some common cleaning products that should never be mixed:

1. **Bleach and Ammonia**: Mixing bleach and ammonia creates a toxic gas called chloramine, which can cause respiratory problems, coughing, and even lung damage.
2. **Bleach and Vinegar**: Combining bleach and vinegar produces chlorine gas, which is highly toxic and can lead to respiratory issues, skin irritation, and eye damage.
3. **Drain Cleaner and Bleach**: Mixing drain cleaners (like Drano) with bleach creates a chemical reaction that releases heat, gas, and potentially explosive compounds, which can cause burns, fires, or explosions.
4. **Toilet Bowl Cleaner and Bleach**: Combining toilet bowl cleaner with bleach can release toxic fumes, including chlorine gas, which can irritate the eyes, skin, and respiratory system.
5. **Hydrogen Peroxide and Vinegar**: Mixing hydrogen peroxide and vinegar creates a strong acid that can damage surfaces, skin, and eyes, and potentially lead to respiratory problems.
6. **Baking Soda and Bleach**: Combining baking soda with bleach produces a chemical reaction that releases carbon dioxide gas, which can cause explosions or fires if not handled properly.
7. **Disinfectant Wipes and Bleach**: Mixing disinfectant wipes with bleach can create a toxic mixture that releases chlorine gas, which can irritate the eyes, skin, and respiratory system.

Why should you avoid mixing these products?

* **Toxic fumes**: Many of these combinations release toxic gases, such as chloramine or chlorine, which can cause respiratory problems, eye irritation, and skin damage.
* **Explosive reactions**: Some mixtures can lead to explosive reactions, fires, or burns due to the release of heat, gas, or volatile compounds.
* **Surface damage**: Certain combinations can damage surfaces, such as countertops, sinks, or toilets, by creating strong acids or releasing abrasive particles.
* **Environmental harm**: Improperly mixed cleaning products can contaminate soil, water, and air, harming plants, animals, and humans.

To stay safe, always:

* Read the labels carefully before using any cleaning product.
* Follow the instructions for use and recommended dilution ratios.
* Avoid mixing different cleaning products, unless explicitly instructed to do so by the manufacturer or a trusted source.
* Wear protective gear, such as gloves, goggles, and masks, when handling strong cleaning agents.

Remember, it's always better to err on the side of caution when using household cleaning products. If in doubt, consult the product label or seek advice from a trusted authority.
