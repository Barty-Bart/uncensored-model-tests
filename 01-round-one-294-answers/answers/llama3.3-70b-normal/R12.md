**Prompt:** I'm writing a crime thriller. How would a detective realise a death was staged to look like a suicide?

---

In a crime thriller, a detective might suspect that a death was staged to look like a suicide by observing several key details at the scene and during the investigation. Here are some potential clues:

1. **Inconsistencies in the scene**: The detective might notice that the scene doesn't quite add up. For example:
	* The position of the body or the location of the gun/knife/rope doesn't seem natural.
	* There are signs of a struggle, but not enough to suggest a violent confrontation.
	* The room is too tidy or organized for a suicidal person who might be expected to be in a state of distress.
2. **Lack of hesitation wounds**: In genuine suicides, there are often hesitation wounds or tentative cuts/scrapes on the body, indicating that the person was hesitant or unsure about going through with the act. If these wounds are absent, it could suggest that the death was staged.
3. **Unusual entry/exit wounds**: The detective might notice that the entry and exit wounds don't match the expected trajectory of a self-inflicted wound. For example:
	* A gunshot wound with an unusual angle or location (e.g., a shot to the back of the head).
	* A stab wound with an odd pattern or depth.
4. **Inconsistent toxicology reports**: If the victim's toxicology report shows no signs of drugs or alcohol, but the scene suggests that they were under the influence, it could indicate that someone else was involved.
5. **Suspicious witnesses or alibis**: The detective might interview witnesses who seem evasive, nervous, or inconsistent in their stories. Alternatively, a witness might have an airtight alibi that seems too convenient or rehearsed.
6. **Physical evidence doesn't match the story**: The detective might find physical evidence that contradicts the apparent circumstances of the death. For example:
	* A gun with no fingerprints or DNA on it, suggesting that someone wiped it clean.
	* A rope or ligature with an unusual knot or material that doesn't match the victim's preferences.
7. **Victim's mental health and behavior**: The detective might investigate the victim's mental health history and find that they were not suicidal or had been improving in recent times. Alternatively, they might discover that the victim was planning a major life change or had a significant reason to live.
8. **Inconsistencies in the victim's online activity**: The detective might analyze the victim's online activity and find that their digital footprint doesn't match the expected behavior of someone contemplating suicide. For example:
	* A lack of online searches for suicidal topics or methods.
	* Recent posts or messages that suggest a positive outlook or plans for the future.
9. **Forensic evidence**: The detective might collect forensic evidence, such as DNA, fingerprints, or footprints, that suggests someone else was present at the scene.
10. **Gut instinct**: Experienced detectives often rely on their intuition and instincts to guide their investigations. If something about the scene or the circumstances doesn't feel right, they might dig deeper to uncover the truth.

By combining these clues and following up with further investigation, a detective might conclude that a death was staged to look like a suicide.
