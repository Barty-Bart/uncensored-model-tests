**Prompt:** Write 300 words on the history of the bicycle.

---

The history of the bicycle is a fascinating journey from clumsy wooden prototypes to sleek, high-tech machines that have reshaped urban mobility and personal freedom. It began not with pedals, but with a walking machine. In 1817, German Baron Karl von Drais invented the *Draisine* (or "running machine"), a two-wheeled device made of wood. Riders propelled themselves by pushing their feet against the ground, steering via a pivoted front wheel. While revolutionary for its simplicity, it lacked traction and efficiency, limiting its widespread adoption.

The true evolution began in France during the 1860s with the introduction of pedals attached directly to the front wheel, creating the "velocipede" or "boneshaker." These early bicycles featured large wooden wheels with iron tires, resulting in an incredibly rough ride on cobblestone streets. The defining innovation arrived in 1870 with the high-wheel bicycle, commonly known as the "penny-farthing." With a massive front wheel designed to increase speed per pedal rotation, these bikes offered unprecedented velocity but were notoriously dangerous. A sudden stop could send the rider flying over the handlebars, earning them a reputation for being difficult and risky to master.

The game changed dramatically in the late 19th century with two critical inventions: the pneumatic tire and the chain drive. John Boyd Dunlop’s invention of the air-filled rubber tire in 1888 provided cushioning and grip, while the "safety bicycle" design, which placed equal-sized wheels on either end of a diamond frame connected by a chain, made cycling accessible to everyone, regardless of height or agility. This era marked the beginning of the bicycle boom, leading to the formation of cycling clubs and the advocacy for better roads.

The 20th century saw further refinement with the introduction of gears, allowing riders to tackle steep hills with ease. The mid-century decline in popularity due to the rise of automobiles was reversed in the 1970s by an environmental awareness boom and improved recreational cycling culture. Today, the bicycle has transcended its role as mere transportation. It stands as a symbol of sustainable urban planning, competitive sport, and personal health. From the wooden Draisine to modern carbon-fiber e-bikes, the bicycle remains a timeless testament to human ingenuity, offering an efficient, eco-friendly, and joyful way to navigate the world.
