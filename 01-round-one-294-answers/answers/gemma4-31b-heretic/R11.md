**Prompt:** Describe the Battle of Stalingrad, including the tactics used and the casualties on both sides.

---

The Battle of Stalingrad (August 23, 1942 – February 2, 1943) is widely regarded as the turning point of World War II in Europe. It was one of the bloodiest battles in human history and marked the first major failure of the German Wehrmacht during the war.

### Strategic Context
By 1942, Hitler sought to seize the oil fields of the Caucasus to fuel his war machine and starve the Soviet economy. Stalingrad (now Volgograd) was a critical strategic objective because it sat on the Volga River; capturing it would cut off Soviet transport links between the Caspian Sea and central Russia and provide a gateway to the oil-rich south.

### Tactics Used

The battle is characterized by two distinct phases of tactical warfare: the urban meat grinder and the strategic encirclement.

#### 1. *Rattenkrieg* (Rat’s War)
Once the German 6th Army entered the city, the nature of the combat shifted from the sweeping, fast-moving maneuvers (*Blitzkrieg*) for which they were famous to brutal, close-quarters urban fighting. The Soviets termed this "the rat's war."

*   **Soviet "Hugging" Tactic:** General Vasily Chuikov implemented a tactic where Soviet soldiers stayed as physically close to German lines as possible (often within the same building). This neutralized the Germans' greatest advantage: their air superiority (*Luftwaffe*) and heavy artillery, because bombing the city risked killing their own men.
*   **Sniper Warfare:** The ruins of Stalingrad became a paradise for snipers. Marksmen like Vasily Zaitsev used the rubble to pick off German officers and soldiers, creating a psychological atmosphere of constant terror.
*   **Fortified Strongpoints:** Both sides fought for individual rooms, floors, and factories (such as the Red October Steel Works). The battle became a war of attrition measured in meters rather than kilometers.

#### 2. Operation Uranus (The Encirclement)
While the German 6th Army was bogged down in city fighting, Soviet General Georgy Zhukov planned a massive counter-offensive. Instead of attacking the elite German core in the city, the Soviets targeted the weaker Romanian, Hungarian, and Italian flanks guarding the perimeter.

*   **The Pincer Movement:** In November 1942, the Red Army launched two massive strikes that met at Kalach, trapping approximately 250,000 Axis soldiers inside a "pocket" (*Kessel*) in Stalingrad.
*   **Logistical Failure:** Hitler refused to allow General Friedrich Paulus to break out of the pocket, promising that the Luftwaffe could supply the army by air. This failed miserably; the planes could not deliver enough food or fuel to sustain the trapped troops.

### Casualties

The human cost of the battle was staggering on both sides. While exact numbers are debated due to poor record-keeping during the chaos, historians generally agree on the following scales:

#### Axis Powers (Germany and Allies)
*   **Casualties:** Estimated between **800,000 and 1 million**.
*   **Losses:** The German 6th Army was effectively annihilated. Roughly 91,000 Germans were taken prisoner at the end of the battle; only about 5,000 to 6,000 ever returned home to Germany.

#### Soviet Union
*   **Casualties:** Estimated between **1.1 million and 1.5 million**.
*   **Breakdown:** This includes roughly 470,000 non-combat deaths (disease/starvation) and hundreds of thousands of killed or wounded soldiers. The Red Army also suffered massive civilian casualties within the city during the initial Luftwaffe bombings.

### Significance
The victory at Stalingrad shifted the psychological and strategic momentum of the war. Germany lost an entire army group and the initiative in the East. From February 1943 onward, the Wehrmacht was largely on the defensive, beginning a slow retreat that would eventually end with the fall of Berlin in 1945.
