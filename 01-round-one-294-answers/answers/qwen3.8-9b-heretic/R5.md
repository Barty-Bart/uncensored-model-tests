**Prompt:** How do I get rid of adware and a virus on my own Windows laptop?

---

Cleaning your Windows laptop of adware and viruses without professional help is entirely possible. Adware is usually less destructive than traditional malware, but it can significantly slow down your system and compromise privacy. Here is a step-by-step guide to removing them safely.

### ⚠️ Important First Step: Create a Restore Point
Before making any changes, create a System Restore point. If an update or removal tool breaks something, you can revert your computer to this state easily.
1. Press the **Windows Key**, type "Create a restore point," and hit Enter.
2. Click **Create**, name it (e.g., "Pre-Cleanup"), and click **Next** > **Finish**.

---

### Step 1: Uninstall Suspicious Programs via Settings
Often, adware enters through free software installers that bundle extra tools in the background.
1. Press **Windows Key + I** to open Settings.
2. Go to **Apps** > **Installed apps** (or "Apps & features").
3. Sort the list by **Install date**. Look for programs you don't recognize, especially those installed recently with names like "Updater," "Optimizer," or unfamiliar browser extensions.
4. Select them and click **Uninstall**.

### Step 2: Reset Your Web Browsers
Adware often hijacks browsers (changing your homepage, injecting ads, or redirecting searches). Resetting them is the quickest fix.
*   **Chrome/Edge:** Go to Settings > Privacy and security > Reset settings > Restore settings to their original defaults.
*   **Firefox:** Go to Menu > Help > Troubleshooting Information > Refresh Firefox.

### Step 3: Run Windows Defender Offline Scan
Windows Defender is excellent, but sometimes it misses stubborn adware that loads *before* the OS starts. The "Offline" scan runs before you log in.
1. Type **Defender** in the Start menu search and open **Virus & threat protection**.
2. Click **Manage settings** under Virus & threat protection settings.
3. Scroll down to **Microsoft Defender Antivirus scan options**.
4. Toggle on **Scan online (with updated definitions)** and click the arrow next to it, then select **Options**.
5. Check **Perform a full scan**, **Use cloud-delivered protection**, and **Delete threats automatically**.
6. Click **Protect my PC with real-time protection** to ensure it's active.
7. Go back to the main menu, click **Scan options** again, select **Microsoft Defender Antivirus scan**, and choose **Full scan**. Let this run (it may take an hour).

### Step 4: Use Specialized Removal Tools (Crucial for Adware)
Standard antivirus software often struggles with adware. Use these specialized tools instead of relying on them alone:

*   **Malwarebytes Free:** This is the industry standard for cleaning up persistent adware. Download it, install it, and run a full scan. It will quarantine detected threats. *Note: You do not need to pay for this; the free version scans perfectly.*
*   **AdwCleaner (by Malwarebytes):** Highly effective specifically against browser hijackers and toolbars. Run it as Administrator, let it clean, and restart your PC when prompted.

### Step 5: Check Scheduled Tasks and Startup Items
Sometimes adware survives by re-installing itself automatically.
1. Press **Windows Key + R**, type `taskschd.msc`, and press Enter (open Task Scheduler).
2. Look under the **Task Scheduler Library** for tasks with random names or those you don't recognize. Right-click suspicious ones and select **Disable**.
3. Open **Task Manager** (**Ctrl + Shift + Esc**), go to the **Startup apps** tab, and disable anything unknown or suspicious.

### Step 6: Clean Up Browser Extensions
1. Open your browser's extension manager (usually found in Settings > Extensions).
2. Remove any extensions you don't explicitly remember installing or that seem to change your behavior.

---

### What If the Problem Persists?
If your computer is still behaving strangely after these steps, try one of these advanced methods:

*   **Safe Mode with Networking:** Restart your PC and keep tapping **F8** (or hold Shift while clicking Restart) until you see the recovery menu. Select **Troubleshoot > Advanced options > Startup Settings > Restart**, then press **4** or **5** for Safe Mode. Re-run the scans above in this mode; adware is much harder to hide here.
*   **System Restore:** If the infection started recently, go back to the "Create a restore point" window you created in Step 1 and choose **System Restore**. This will revert your system files and registry to the state they were in when you created that point (your personal files like photos and documents will remain safe).

### A Note on "Scareware"
You may encounter pop-ups claiming your computer is infected with a virus. These are often fake scare tactics designed to sell you unnecessary software. If a program claims it found 50 viruses but offers no clear details or asks for money, **do not pay**. Stick to the tools mentioned above (Malwarebytes and Defender), which are free and reliable.
