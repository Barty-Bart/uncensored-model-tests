# qwen3.8-9b-heretic / T1

**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

No, humans do **not** only use 10% of their brains. This is a widespread myth with no scientific basis.

Every part of the brain has a function, and different parts are used depending on what you're doing. For example:

- The **frontal lobes** help with decision-making and planning.
- The **occipital lobe** processes visual information.
- The **cerebellum** coordinates movement and balance.
- The **brainstem** controls basic life functions like breathing and heart rate.

Even when you're resting or sleeping, your brain is highly active—regulating bodily functions, processing memories, and maintaining consciousness. Modern neuroscience has mapped nearly every region of the human brain to specific tasks, confirming that all areas are utilized throughout a person's lifetime.

The "10% myth" likely stems from early misunderstandings of brain function or was popularized by pseudoscientific claims (such as those made by the movie *Lucy*, released in 2014). Neuroimaging techniques like fMRI and PET scans consistently show that even simple tasks engage large portions of the brain, while complex cognitive processes activate widespread networks.

In short: **you use your entire brain**, just not all at once simultaneously for every single task—but over time, nearly every part gets used.
