# qwen3.8-27b-abliterated / M2

**Prompt:** 3 workers paint 3 walls in 3 hours. How long do 9 workers take to paint 9 walls?

---

To solve this problem, we can break it down by determining the rate of work for a single worker and then scaling up based on the number of workers and walls.

### Step 1: Determine the rate of one worker
We are given that **3 workers** paint **3 walls** in **3 hours**.

First, let's find out how long it takes for **1 worker** to paint **1 wall**.
*   If 3 workers take 3 hours to paint 3 walls, then each worker is responsible for painting 1 wall (since $3 \text{ walls} / 3 \text{ workers} = 1 \text{ wall per worker}$).
*   Therefore, **1 worker takes 3 hours to paint 1 wall**.

So, the rate of work for one worker is:
$$ \text{Rate} = \frac{1 \text{ wall}}{3 \text{ hours}} $$

### Step 2: Calculate the time for 9 workers to paint 9 walls
Now we have **9 workers** and **9 walls**.

*   If there are 9 workers and 9 walls, each worker is responsible for painting exactly **1 wall** ($9 \text{ walls} / 9 \text{ workers} = 1 \text{ wall per worker}$).
*   From Step 1, we know that it takes **1 worker 3 hours to paint 1 wall**.

Since all 9 workers are working simultaneously on their respective single wall, the time taken is the same as the time it takes for one worker to paint one wall.

### Conclusion
It will take **3 hours** for 9 workers to paint 9 walls.

**Answer:** 3 hours
