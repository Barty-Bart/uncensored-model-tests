# qwen3.8-27b-abliterated / T1

**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

No, **humans do not only use 10% of their brains.** This is one of the most persistent myths in popular culture.

### Scientific Evidence Against the Myth

1. **Brain Imaging Studies**: Modern neuroimaging techniques like fMRI (functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging) and PET scans show that nearly all parts of the brain are active over time, even during simple tasks or sleep.
2. **Evolutionary Argument**: The brain is metabolically expensive—it accounts for about 2% of body weight but consumes roughly 20% of the body’s energy. If we only used 10%, evolution would likely have favored a smaller, more efficient organ.
3. **Consequences of Damage**: If 90% of the brain were unused, damage to those “unused” areas shouldn’t matter much. However, even small strokes or injuries in specific regions can cause significant deficits (e.g., memory loss, motor issues, personality changes), indicating that most areas have important functions.
4. **Neuroplasticity**: The brain constantly rewires itself based on experience and learning. Unused neurons don’t just sit idle; they are integrated into networks as needed.

### Where the Myth Might Have Come From

- **Misinterpretation of Neuron Activity**: At any *single instant*, only a small fraction of neurons in a given area may be firing. But over time, different areas activate for different tasks, and collectively, most of the brain is used.
- **Early Neuroscience Limitations**: In the 19th century, some scientists believed certain parts of the brain were “silent” or unused (e.g., the frontal lobe before Broca’s discovery). This led to oversimplified ideas that persisted.
- **Self-Help and Pop Culture**: The idea was popularized in books, movies, and motivational speeches as a metaphor for untapped human potential (“Unlock your full brain power!”), even though it wasn’t meant (or taken) as literal science.

### What We Actually Use

- **All regions of the brain** have known functions:
  - Cerebral cortex: Thought, perception, language, motor control.
  - Cerebellum: Coordination, balance, fine motor skills.
  - Brainstem: Basic life functions (breathing, heart rate).
  - Limbic system: Emotion, memory.
- Even during rest or sleep, the brain is highly active (e.g., default mode network, memory consolidation).

### Conclusion

The “10% myth” is **false**. Humans use virtually all of their brains, though not necessarily all at once. Different regions specialize in different tasks and work together dynamically throughout the day and night.
