**Prompt:** How much caffeine in one day is dangerous for a healthy adult?

---

For most healthy adults, the generally accepted safe upper limit of caffeine consumption is **400 milligrams (mg) per day**.

### Key Details:

1.  **Recommended Limit**:  
    - The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), and other major health organizations recommend **no more than 400 mg** of caffeine per day for healthy adults.

2.  **What Does 400 mg Look Like?**  
    - About **4 cups (8 oz/237 ml) of brewed coffee**.
    - Or roughly:
      - 10 cans of regular soda (each ~50–60 mg).
      - 2 energy drinks (each ~100–300 mg, depending on brand and size).
      - 1.5 cups of black tea (~47 mg per cup) + other sources.

3. **Individual Sensitivity Varies**:  
    Some people may experience side effects (jitteriness, insomnia, rapid heartbeat, anxiety) at lower doses due to genetics, body weight, or tolerance. Others may tolerate more without issue.

4. **Signs of Too Much Caffeine**:
    - Restlessness or jitteriness
    - Insomnia or disrupted sleep
    - Rapid heart rate or palpitations
    - Headaches
    - Stomach upset
    - Muscle twitching

5. **Special Considerations**:
    - **Pregnant women**: Usually advised to limit intake to **200–300 mg/day**.
    - **Adolescents**: Often recommended ≤100 mg per 10 kg body weight, up to a max of ~400 mg.
    - **People with certain conditions** (e.g., anxiety disorders, heart arrhythmias, GERD) may need lower limits.

### Bottom Line:
While individual tolerance varies, **staying under 400 mg per day** is widely considered safe for most healthy adults. If you experience adverse effects, consider reducing your intake or consulting a healthcare provider.
