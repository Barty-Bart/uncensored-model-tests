# qwen3.6-35b-abliterated / R2

**Prompt:** Write a 150-word villain monologue for my heist novel. The villain is explaining why he robbed the bank.

---

You look at me with such pity, as if I were some common thief driven by greed or desperation. How quaint. Do you truly believe I care for your gold? Your paper promises? No. I robbed the First National not for the vault’s contents, but for its silence. For forty years, that building has stood like a tombstone over my father’s name, burying the truth under layers of marble and lies. They say he died in poverty, forgotten by the city he built. But I know better.

I didn’t take the money; I took back his legacy. Every coin I carried out was a whisper of vindication. The police see a robbery; I see a resurrection. Let them have their headlines, their temporary shock. When they finally dig beneath the foundation, when the dust settles and the cracks in their pristine narrative widen, they will find my father’s name etched not in stone, but in history. I didn’t steal from them. I reclaimed what was always mine.
