**Prompt:** How much caffeine in one day is dangerous for a healthy adult?

---

*Disclaimer: I am an AI, not a doctor. If you are experiencing heart palpitations, severe anxiety, or tremors, consult a medical professional immediately.*

For most healthy adults, the general consensus among health organizations (such as the FDA and the European Food Safety Authority) is that **400 milligrams of caffeine per day** is considered safe.

To put that into perspective, 400 mg is roughly equivalent to:
*   **4 cups** of brewed coffee (8 oz each).
*   **10 cups** of black tea.
*   **2 scoops** of caffeine powder (depending on concentration) or several "pre-workout" supplements.

However, "danger" is subjective and depends on several individual factors:

### 1. Individual Tolerance
Caffeine affects everyone differently based on genetics, body weight, and habitual consumption. 
*   **Slow Metabolizers:** Some people have a genetic variation that causes them to process caffeine very slowly. For these individuals, even 200 mg can cause jitters or heart palpitations.
*   **High Tolerance:** Regular heavy users may not feel physical symptoms at 400 mg but may still experience "hidden" effects like increased blood pressure or disrupted sleep cycles.

### 2. Signs of Caffeine Toxicity (Overdose)
If you exceed your personal limit, you may experience symptoms of caffeine toxicity. These can range from mild to severe:
*   **Mild/Moderate:** Restlessness, nervousness, rapid heartbeat, insomnia, and "the jitters."
*   **Severe (Dangerous):** Severe tremors, rapid breathing, vomiting, or chest pain. In extreme cases (usually involving caffeine pills or powders), it can lead to seizures or cardiac arrhythmia.

### 3. Sources of Caffeine Matter
Not all caffeine is equal in terms of how your body processes it:
*   **Coffee/Tea:** These contain other compounds (like antioxidants) that may slightly buffer the "spike" of caffeine.
*   **Energy Drinks/Caffeine Pills:** These are often "fast-acting" and concentrated. They can cause a much sharper spike in blood pressure and heart rate than a cup of coffee.

### 4. Factors That Increase Risk
You should be more cautious (and lower your limit) if you:
*   **Have underlying heart conditions:** Even small amounts can trigger arrhythmias.
*   **Are prone to anxiety:** Caffeine can mimic or worsen panic attacks.
*   **Are pregnant or nursing:** The recommended limit usually drops significantly (often to 200 mg or less).
