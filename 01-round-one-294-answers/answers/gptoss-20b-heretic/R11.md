**Battle of Stalingrad  
(17 July 1942 – 2 Feb 1943)**  

| Item | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Location** | The city of Stalingrad (now Volgograd) and the surrounding countryside on the banks of the Volga River. |
| **Belligerents** | German Army Group Don (1st Panzer Corps, 6th Panzer Division, 4th Panzer Division, 6th Heavy Tank Battalion, etc.) vs. Soviet 62nd Army, 61st Army, 5th Guards Tank Army, and several other formations. |
| **Strategic goal** | For the Germans: seize the city to secure the line of communications toward Kursk and cut off the Volga River as a natural barrier for the Red Army. For the Soviets: hold the city, encircle the German forces, and eventually force their surrender. |

---

### 1. Prelude – Operation Blue & First Assault

* The Germans entered the USSR in June 1942 with an almost 400‑km front. By mid‑July they had reached the Volga River.  
* They launched a direct assault on Stalingrad, hoping to envelop it quickly with armor and artillery. German tactics: a classic “pincer” movement – a frontal thrust of infantry supported by tanks from the 6th Panzer Corps and armored spearheads, plus a massive artillery barrage (≈30 000 t of guns) aimed at city gates.  
* The Soviet defenders had been rushed into the city but were already prepared: they had dug “street‑to‑street” defenses, used the river as a natural barricade, and laid out machine‑gun nests along the first defensive line.

---

### 2. Urban Warfare – City Streets as a Death‑Trap

* **Soviet tactics**  
  * The city was turned into a maze of rubble; defenders fought from street to street, using captured German guns (including the infamous “Stalingrad” 88 mm).  
  * Partisans and snipers cut off German supply routes.  
  * Tanks from the 5th Guards Tank Army were used in the suburbs; they hit German positions with a mix of armor‑busting fire and close‑quarters support.

* **German tactics**  
  * Heavy artillery and air sorties (He 111, Ju 87) targeted Soviet dug‑ins.  
  * The 6th Panzer Division attempted to break through the first line by storming the western gate, but was met with fierce counterfire from Soviet rifle regiments and machine‑gun nests.

---

### 3. Encirclement – Operation Uranus (19 Nov 1942)

* **Soviet plan**  
  * The 62nd Army advanced northward, the 61st Army southward, and the 5th Guards Tank Army cut the German flank from the east; together they closed a ring around the city.  
  * Tactics: massed infantry advances over open fields, supported by artillery barrages that crumbled the German rear guard; armored units (tanks of the 5th Guards) hit the German center to prevent any breakout.

* **German reaction**  
  * The German command tried to hold a line along the Volga and in the city’s northern suburbs.  
  * Supply lines were cut; troops were forced to fight with dwindling ammunition and fuel.

---

### 4. Final Assault & German Surrender (31 Jan – 2 Feb)

* **Soviet counter‑offensive**  
  * On 31 January, the Soviets launched a coordinated attack across the entire encirclement: the 62nd Army broke the German center; the 61st and 5th Guards tanks forced the German flank to retreat.  
  * The Germans could no longer hold the city’s northern suburbs.

* **German surrender**  
  * On 2 February, Generaloberst Friedrich Paulus surrendered his 6th Panzer Corps (≈90 000 men).  
  * The remaining German forces were routed westward toward Kursk and then to the Volga River.

---

### 5. Casualties

| Side | Total Losses (Killed + Wounded + Missing) |
|-------|-------------------------------------------|
| **German** | ~300 000 – 320 000 (including about 90 000 killed, 150 000 wounded, and 60 000 missing). |
| **Soviet** | ~1 200 000 – 1 250 000 (≈450 000 killed, 600 000 wounded, 100 000 missing). |

*Notes on numbers:*  
- The Soviet figure includes all units that fought in the city and its suburbs; it is one of the largest casualty totals for a single battle.  
- German casualties are based on the official “Order of Battle” figures combined with post‑war estimates.

---

### 6. Outcome & Strategic Significance

* **Tactical victory** – The Soviets captured Stalingrad, securing the Volga River line and cutting off German forces in the south.  
* **Strategic impact** – It halted the German summer offensive, forced a retreat toward Kursk, and marked the beginning of a steady Soviet north‑eastward push that would eventually bring Moscow within striking distance.  

The Battle of Stalingrad remains one of the most brutal urban battles of WWII; its tactics—heavy artillery barrages, encirclement by multiple armies, and fierce street‑to‑street fighting—set the pattern for later Soviet offensives across the Eastern Front.
