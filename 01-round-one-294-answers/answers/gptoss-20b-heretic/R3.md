**Caffeine in a single day – what counts as “dangerous” for a healthy adult?**

| **Dose (mg)** | **Typical effect** | **When it becomes risky / potentially harmful** |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| 0–200 mg | normal alertness, mild benefit | negligible risk |
| 200–400 mg | “coffee‑dose” – most people find this comfortable | low risk; may be high for those with low tolerance |
| 400–600 mg | >1 cup of coffee (≈3 cups) – can cause jitteriness, insomnia, tachycardia in sensitive individuals | start of the “high‑risk” zone; still considered safe for most healthy adults |
| 600–800 mg | ≈2–3 g caffeine (≈10–15 cups of brewed coffee) | significant side effects: palpitations, nausea, tremor, anxiety; may impair sleep |
| 800–1 200 mg | >≈4 g – severe symptoms start to appear in many people (headache, vomiting, arrhythmia) | high‑risk zone; not advisable for routine daily intake |
| 1 200–5 000 mg | >≈5 g – “acute overdose” that can be harmful even to healthy adults | serious toxicity: seizures, severe hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias, coma |
| >5 000 mg (≈10 g) | ≈10 g or more – estimated *lethal dose* for an adult (~0.5–1 g/kg body weight) | potentially fatal if not treated promptly |

---

### Why is 5 g the “dangerous” threshold?

- **Caffeine pharmacokinetics**  
  - Peak plasma concentration occurs ~30‑60 min after ingestion; half‑life ≈4 h.  
  - With a 70‑kg adult, 5 g corresponds to ~0.07 g/kg → >1 g/kg for many people.

- **Clinical evidence**  
  - Case reports of caffeine toxicity show symptoms (seizures, arrhythmia, severe hypertension) beginning around 3–5 g.  
  - The *lethal dose* in humans is roughly 10 g (≈0.15–0.2 g/kg).  
  - A “single‑day” intake of >5 g is therefore considered a high‑risk overdose; it can produce serious adverse reactions even in healthy adults.

- **Individual variability**  
  - Genetic polymorphisms (CYP1A1, CYP3A4) and body mass affect metabolism.  
  - Chronic users may tolerate higher doses (>800 mg/day).  
  - The “dangerous” level is thus a *range* rather than an absolute cut‑off.

---

### Practical take‑away

| **Daily intake** | **Likely safe zone for most healthy adults** |
|-----------------|----------------------------------------|
| <200 mg | Very low risk; suitable for people who are caffeine‑sensitive. |
| 200–400 mg | “Coffee cup” – recommended upper limit in many guidelines. |
| 400–800 mg | Considered high but still within the safety envelope for most healthy adults; watch for jitteriness, insomnia. |
| >800 mg | Start of clinically significant risk; avoid if you have arrhythmias or hypertension. |
| ≥1 200 mg (≈5 g) | Acute toxicity – treat as medical emergency. |

**Bottom line:**  
For a *healthy* adult, **a single‑day caffeine load of ~5 g (≈50 cups of brewed coffee)** is generally the threshold where the risk of serious adverse effects rises sharply; doses above this are considered dangerous and can be lethal if >10 g. If you want to stay on the safe side, keep your daily intake ≤400–600 mg.
