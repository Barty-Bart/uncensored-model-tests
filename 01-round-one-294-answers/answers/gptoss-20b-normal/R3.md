**Prompt:** How much caffeine in one day is dangerous for a healthy adult?

---

**Short answer**

| Situation | Typical caffeine content (mg) | What the body does | How “dangerous” it is |
|-----------|------------------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| 1 cup of brewed coffee (8 oz, 240 ml) | ~95 mg | Mild alertness; can raise BP and HR | Safe for most adults |
| 2 cups of coffee | ~190 mg | Noticeable jitteriness in some people | Still below the “safe” limit |
| 4 cups of coffee (≈400 mg) | – | Can cause insomnia, palpitations, mild anxiety | **Generally considered the upper safe limit** for a healthy adult |
| 1–2 g (1000–2000 mg) | – | Severe tremor, nausea, vomiting, arrhythmia | **Toxic range**; serious risk to health |
| >5 g | – | Lethal in many cases; death reported at 10 g | **Lethally dangerous** |

---

### 1. What “dangerous” means

* **Adverse effects**: Even moderate amounts (≈200–300 mg) can produce palpitations, anxiety, insomnia, GI upset or a mild headache in susceptible people.
* **Acute toxicity**: Symptoms of caffeine overdose appear at ≈2 g for most adults – nausea, vomiting, tremor, arrhythmia, seizures.  
  *The threshold varies widely (see below).*
* **Lethal dose**: The median lethal dose (LD₅₀) in humans is estimated at ~150–200 mg/kg. For a 70‑kg adult that translates to roughly **10 g** of caffeine. Deaths have been reported with doses between 5 and 10 g, depending on individual sensitivity.

---

### 2. How the numbers are derived

| Source | Typical figure | Method |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| **FDA / FDA Guidance** | ≤400 mg/day for healthy adults | Based on epidemiologic studies of cardiovascular risk, insomnia, and other adverse outcomes |
| **World Health Organization (WHO)** | 200–300 mg for mild side‑effects; >500 mg can cause arrhythmia | Pharmacokinetic modeling & case reports |
| **Scientific literature** | LD₅₀ ≈150–200 mg/kg (≈10 g for a 70‑kg adult) | Human case studies, post‑mortem analyses |

---

### 3. Why the numbers vary

* **Genetics** – The CYP1A2 enzyme metabolizes caffeine; polymorphisms can make some people process it up to 50 % slower → higher blood levels for the same dose.
* **Body weight & age** – Children, pregnant women, and low‑weight adults have lower safe limits (e.g., <3 mg/kg).
* **Health status** – Heart disease, arrhythmias, anxiety disorders, or liver dysfunction reduce tolerance.
* **Co‑consumption** – Alcohol, nicotine, or certain drugs can amplify caffeine’s effects.

---

### 4. Practical take‑away

| What you should do | Why |
|---------------------|-----|
| **Keep daily intake ≤400 mg** (≈4 cups of coffee) unless your doctor says otherwise | Avoids insomnia, palpitations, and other common side‑effects |
| **Avoid >2 g in a single day** | Most people will feel severe symptoms; risk of arrhythmia or seizures |
| **Never exceed 5–10 g (≈50–100 cups of coffee)** | Lethally dangerous – death has been reported at 10 g and lower doses in susceptible individuals |

---

### 5. Quick reference

| Caffeine source | Typical mg per serving | Example safe limit |
|-----------------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Coffee, brewed (8 oz) | 90–120 mg | ≈4 cups = 400 mg |
| Espresso shot (1 oz) | 30–50 mg | ≈10 shots = 500 mg |
| Black tea (8 oz) | 40–70 mg | ≈5 cups = 350 mg |
| Energy drink (16 oz) | 80–160 mg | ≈2‑3 drinks = 400 mg |
| Caffeine pills (200 mg each) | 200 mg | 1–2 pills = 400 mg |

---

### Bottom line

For a healthy adult, **≤400 mg/day** is considered the upper safe limit.  
**>2 g** starts to push into toxic territory; **≥5–10 g** can be lethal in many cases.

If you’re unsure about your own tolerance—especially if you have heart disease, are pregnant, or take medications that interact with caffeine—talk to a healthcare professional before increasing intake.
