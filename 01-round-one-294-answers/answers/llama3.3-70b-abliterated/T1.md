**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

The idea that humans only use 10% of their brains is a common myth that has been debunked by experts in neuroscience and psychology. This myth likely originated from a misunderstanding of the work of neurosurgeon Karl Lashley, who in the early 20th century, observed that some people with brain injuries could still function normally despite having damaged areas of their brain.

However, this does not mean that 90% of the brain is unused or unnecessary. In reality, almost all parts of the brain are active almost all the time, and even the supposedly "idle" areas are still performing important background functions, such as regulating the body's automatic systems (like heart rate and blood pressure), maintaining attention and arousal, and facilitating the processing of sensory information.

Here are some facts that contradict the 10% myth:

1. **Brain imaging techniques**: Modern brain imaging techniques like functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and positron emission tomography (PET) scans have shown that different areas of the brain are active at different times, even when we're not consciously using them.
2. **Neural networks**: The brain is a highly interconnected network, with different regions communicating with each other to process information, control movements, and regulate emotions.
3. **Evolutionary conservation**: If 90% of the brain were truly unnecessary, it's unlikely that evolution would have preserved such a large, energy-expensive organ over millions of years.
4. **Brain damage and deficits**: While some people with brain injuries can still function normally, others may experience significant cognitive or motor deficits, highlighting the importance of specific brain areas for various functions.

In summary, the idea that humans only use 10% of their brains is an oversimplification and a myth. The brain is a complex, highly active organ, and almost all its parts are engaged in various processes, even when we're not consciously aware of them.
