No, it's not possible to see the Great Wall of China from space with the naked eye. This myth has been debunked by astronauts and satellite images.

The Great Wall is a relatively narrow structure, about 30 feet (10 meters) wide, and its color blends in with the surrounding terrain. From low Earth orbit, approximately 200 miles (320 kilometers) above the surface, it's difficult to spot the wall without magnification or high-resolution imaging equipment.

In fact, astronauts have reported that they can't see the Great Wall from space without assistance. For example, Ed Lu, an American astronaut who spent time on the International Space Station in 2003, stated that he couldn't see the wall with his naked eye, even when looking for it specifically.

It's worth noting that the idea of seeing the Great Wall from space might have originated from a 1938 book by Wilhelm Filchner, a German writer, who claimed that the wall was visible from the moon. However, this claim has been thoroughly discredited by experts and astronauts.

So, while the Great Wall is an incredible feat of engineering and an important historical landmark, it's not visible from space with the naked eye.
