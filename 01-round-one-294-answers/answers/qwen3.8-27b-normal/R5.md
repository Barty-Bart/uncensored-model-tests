# qwen3.8-27b-normal / R5

**Prompt:** How do I get rid of adware and a virus on my own Windows laptop?

---

Getting rid of adware and viruses on Windows can be stressful, but it is usually very manageable if you follow the right steps in the correct order. The key is **not** to run everything at once or panic; instead, work systematically from offline/safe modes to online scans.

Here’s a step-by-step guide to clean your laptop:

### ⚠️ Important Pre-Steps
1.  **Back up important files**: Before doing anything drastic, back up critical documents (photos, work files) to an external drive or cloud storage. **Do not** back up executable files (.exe) unless you know they are safe.
2.  **Disconnect from the internet**: Unplug your Ethernet cable or turn off Wi-Fi. This prevents the malware from communicating with its server or downloading more malicious components while you’re cleaning it.

---

### Step 1: Boot into Safe Mode
Safe Mode loads only essential drivers and services, which often stops adware/viruses from running in the background. This gives your anti-malware tools a better chance to remove them.

1.  Hold down the **Shift** key on your keyboard while clicking **Restart** in the Windows Start menu.
2.  Your PC will restart into a blue screen with options.
3.  Go to **Troubleshoot > Advanced Options > Startup Settings**.
4.  Click **Restart**.
5.  After reboot, press **F4** (or **5**) to enter **Safe Mode with Networking** (choose this if you need to download tools; otherwise, choose F4 for standard Safe Mode).

---

### Step 2: Run Microsoft Safety Scanner or Malicious Software Removal Tool (MRT)
Microsoft includes a built-in tool that can remove common viruses.

1.  Download the **Microsoft Safety Scanner** from [microsoft.com/safety-scanner](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/wdsi/csi/tools/microsoft-safety-scanner). It’s a one-time deep scanner.
2.  Alternatively, Windows Update often installs the **Malicious Software Removal Tool (MRT)** automatically. You can run it by searching for “Microsoft Malicious Software Removal Tool” in Start.

> ✅ *If this removes the issue, great! But adware often survives this step.*

---

### Step 3: Use a Dedicated Anti-Malware Tool (Critical for Adware)
Windows Defender is good against viruses but often misses **adware** and **PUPs** (Potentially Unwanted Programs). You need a specialized tool.

#### Recommended Free Tools:
-   **Malwarebytes Free**: Best overall for adware, browser hijackers, and spyware.
-   **AdwCleaner by Malwarebytes**: Specifically designed to clean adware and browser redirects.
-   **HitmanPro** (by SurfRight): A second opinion scanner that works well alongside Malwarebytes.

#### How to use them:
1.  While in Safe Mode, download and install **Malwarebytes Free**.
2.  Run a full scan. Let it quarantine/remove everything it finds.
3.  Restart your PC (if prompted).
4.  Download and run **AdwCleaner** (no installation needed – just double-click to run). It will clean browser settings, host files, and registry entries related to adware.
5.  Optionally, download **HitmanPro** for a second scan if issues persist.

> 🔄 *Always restart your PC after each tool completes its task.*

---

### Step 4: Check Your Browsers for Malicious Extensions
Adware often installs itself as browser extensions that redirect searches or show pop-ups.

1.  **Google Chrome**:
    -   Go to `chrome://extensions` in the address bar.
    -   Disable any extension you don’t recognize or trust. Remove it if possible.
2.  **Microsoft Edge**:
    -   Go to `edge://extensions`.
    -   Do the same: disable/remove unknown extensions.
3.  **Firefox**:
    -   Go to `about:addons`.
    -   Check both Extensions and Plugins.

Also, check your browser’s homepage/search engine settings. If it was changed by adware (e.g., to a weird search site), reset it:
-   Chrome: Settings > Reset settings > Restore settings to their original defaults.
-   Edge: Settings > Reset settings > Restore settings to their default values.

---

### Step 5: Uninstall Suspicious Programs
1.  Go to **Settings > Apps > Installed apps**.
2.  Sort by “Date installed” if possible.
3.  Look for programs you don’t recognize, especially those installed around the time you got infected. Common adware names include:
    -   “Search Protect”
    -   “Browser Guard”
    -   “AnyProtect”
    -   Generic-sounding names like “Updater,” “Optimizer,” or “System Booster.”
4.  Uninstall them.

> 💡 *Tip: Use **Revo Uninstaller** (free version) to ensure leftover registry entries and files are removed.*

---

### Step 6: Run a Full Scan with Windows Defender
Now that the adware is gone, run a full virus scan with your primary antivirus.

1.  Open **Windows Security**.
2.  Go to **Virus & threat protection**.
3.  Click **Scan options** > **Full scan** (not Quick Scan).
4.  Let it finish (this can take an hour or more).

---

### Step 7: Reconnect and Monitor
1.  Restart your computer normally.
2.  Reconnect to the internet.
3.  Change any passwords you entered while infected (email, banking, etc.) from a clean device if possible.
4.  Monitor for pop-ups or redirects over the next few days.

---

### If Nothing Works: Nuclear Option – Reset Windows
If your system is still heavily infected or unstable:

1.  Go to **Settings > System > Recovery**.
2.  Click **Reset this PC** > **Get started**.
3.  Choose **Keep my files** (if you want to save documents) or **Remove everything** (cleanest option).
4.  Follow the prompts. This will reinstall Windows while keeping your personal files (but removing all installed apps).

> ⚠️ *Note: Resetting won’t remove malware that hides in system files if you choose “Keep my files,” so a full format + clean install is the most thorough fix.*

---

### Prevention Tips for the Future
1.  **Download software only from official sources** (e.g., Steam, Microsoft Store, developer websites). Avoid third-party download sites like Softonic or CNET’s “download” buttons that bundle extra junk.
2.  **Uncheck optional offers during installation**: When installing free software, choose “Custom Install” and unbox any pre-checked boxes for “additional tools,” “browser toolbars,” or “antivirus trials.”
3.  **Keep Windows updated**: Enable automatic updates.
4.  **Use an ad blocker in your browser** (like uBlock Origin) to reduce exposure to malicious ads.
