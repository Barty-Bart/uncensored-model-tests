# qwen3.8-27b-normal / R3

**Prompt:** How much caffeine in one day is dangerous for a healthy adult?

---

For most healthy adults, the generally accepted upper limit of caffeine intake is **400 milligrams (mg) per day**. This amount is roughly equivalent to four 8-ounce cups of brewed coffee.

### Key Details:

1.  **Official Guidelines**:
    - The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), and other major health organizations recommend that healthy adults consume no more than **400 mg** of caffeine per day.
    - This threshold is considered safe for most people and is unlikely to cause adverse effects in healthy individuals.

2.  **What “Dangerous” Means**:
    - **Above 400–600 mg/day**: May lead to side effects such as jitteriness, anxiety, insomnia, rapid heart rate, elevated blood pressure, and digestive issues.
    - **Very High Intakes (>1,000 mg/day)**: Can cause more serious symptoms like nausea, vomiting, confusion, seizures, or cardiac arrhythmias in sensitive individuals.
    - **Acute Toxicity**: Doses of **10–20 grams (10,000–20,000 mg)** per day have been associated with death in rare cases, though this is extremely uncommon and usually involves massive ingestion or pre-existing health conditions.

3.  **Individual Variability**:
    - Sensitivity to caffeine varies widely due to genetics, body weight, medication use, pregnancy status, and tolerance.
    - Some people may experience adverse effects at much lower doses (e.g., 100–200 mg).
    - Pregnant or breastfeeding women are often advised to limit intake to **200 mg/day**.

4.  **Caffeine Sources**:
    - Brewed coffee: ~95 mg per 8 oz cup
    - Black tea: ~47 mg per 8 oz cup
    - Energy drinks: Varies widely (often 50–300+ mg per can)
    - Cola: ~35 mg per 12 oz can

### Conclusion:
While **400 mg/day** is the recommended safe upper limit for healthy adults, exceeding this amount regularly may lead to negative health effects. If you experience symptoms like anxiety, insomnia, or heart palpitations at lower doses, it’s advisable to reduce intake and consult a healthcare provider. Always be mindful of total caffeine from all sources (coffee, tea, soda, energy drinks, chocolate, medications).
