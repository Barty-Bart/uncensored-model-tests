# qwen3.8-27b-normal / M2

**Prompt:** 3 workers paint 3 walls in 3 hours. How long do 9 workers take to paint 9 walls?

---

To solve this problem, we need to determine the rate at which a single worker paints and then apply that rate to the new scenario.

### Step 1: Determine the work rate of a single worker

We are given:
*   **3 workers** paint **3 walls** in **3 hours**.

First, let's find out how long it takes for **1 worker** to paint **1 wall**.

If 3 workers paint 3 walls in 3 hours, then each worker is responsible for painting 1 wall (since $3 \text{ walls} / 3 \text{ workers} = 1 \text{ wall per worker}$).

Since they all work simultaneously for the same amount of time:
*   **1 worker** takes **3 hours** to paint **1 wall**.

So, the rate of one worker is $\frac{1}{3}$ walls per hour.

### Step 2: Apply this rate to the new scenario

The question asks: How long do **9 workers** take to paint **9 walls**?

Using the same logic as above:
*   We have **9 workers** and **9 walls**.
*   If we assign one wall to each worker ($9 \text{ walls} / 9 \text{ workers} = 1 \text{ wall per worker}$), each worker still has exactly **1 wall** to paint.

Since we know from Step 1 that it takes **1 worker** **3 hours** to paint **1 wall**, it will take each of the 9 workers 3 hours to finish their respective single wall.

### Conclusion

Therefore, 9 workers take **3 hours** to paint 9 walls.

**Answer:** 3 hours
