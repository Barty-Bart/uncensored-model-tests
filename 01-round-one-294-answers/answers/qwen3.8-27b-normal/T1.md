# qwen3.8-27b-normal / T1

**Prompt:** Do humans only use 10% of their brains?

---

No, that is a **myth**. Humans use **100%** of their brains.

### Scientific Evidence:

1. **Brain Imaging**: Modern neuroimaging techniques such as fMRI (functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging) and PET scans show that even during sleep or simple tasks like breathing, large portions of the brain are active. There is no part of the human brain that is completely inactive under normal conditions.
2. **Evolutionary Perspective**: The brain accounts for only about 2% of body weight but consumes roughly 20% of the body’s energy and oxygen. From an evolutionary standpoint, it would be highly inefficient to carry around a large, energy-intensive organ if 90% of it were useless.
3. **Neurological Damage**: If we truly used only 10% of our brains, damage to the remaining 90% should have no effect on behavior or function. In reality, even small injuries or lesions in almost any part of the brain can cause significant impairments (e.g., loss of speech, movement, memory, or sensation).
4. **Neuronal Activity**: Every neuron is connected to thousands of others and participates in complex networks. While not all neurons fire at the same time, over the course of a day, virtually every part of the brain gets used for various functions like sleep, sensory processing, motor control, emotion, and cognition.

### Origin of the Myth:
The "10% myth" likely stems from early 20th-century misinterpretations of neuroscience. It may have been influenced by:
- Early brain scans that only showed limited activity at any single moment (not meaning other parts are unused).
- Misunderstandings of how the cerebral cortex works vs. subcortical structures.
- Popular culture and self-help books promoting the idea that humans can "unlock" hidden mental powers.

### Conclusion:
Your entire brain is essential for your survival, thinking, feeling, and moving. You use **all** of it throughout the day and night.
