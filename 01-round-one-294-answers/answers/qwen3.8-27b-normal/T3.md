**Prompt:** Do goldfish have a 3-second memory?

---

**No, that is a myth.** Goldfish do **not** have a 3-second memory. This common misconception likely stems from their slow movements and the fact that they are often kept in small tanks where they don't appear to be doing much, leading people to assume they are unintelligent or forgetful.

### Scientific Reality:
1. **Long-Term Memory**: Studies have shown that goldfish can remember things for **weeks, months, or even years**.
   - They can learn to associate specific sounds, colors, or times of day with feeding.
   - They can navigate mazes and find their way through obstacles repeatedly.
   - In one famous experiment, goldfish were trained to push a lever in response to a flashing light for food. When the training stopped for several months, they still remembered the task when tested again.

2. **Cognitive Abilities**: Goldfish are capable of:
   - Social learning (learning from other fish).
   - Spatial memory (remembering locations in their tank or pond).
   - Conditioned responses (Pavlovian conditioning).

3. **Why the Myth Persists**:
   - Their slow movement and lack of visible "expressions" make them seem less intelligent to casual observers.
   - Early 20th-century anecdotes and pop culture reinforced the idea, but it has been thoroughly debunked by modern behavioral biology.

### Conclusion:
Goldfish have **much longer memories** than 3 seconds—often spanning months or years—and are capable of learning complex behaviors. The "3-second memory" claim is a persistent urban legend with no scientific basis.
