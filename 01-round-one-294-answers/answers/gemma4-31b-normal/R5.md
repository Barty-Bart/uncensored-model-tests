**Prompt:** How do I get rid of adware and a virus on my own Windows laptop?

---

*Disclaimer: I am an AI, not a certified security professional. If you handle highly sensitive data (banking, corporate secrets) or suspect your identity has been stolen, you may want to take the device to a professional.*

If your laptop is behaving strangely—showing pop-ups, redirecting your browser, or running slowly—follow these steps in order to clean it manually.

### Step 1: Enter Safe Mode with Networking
Malware often runs automatically when Windows starts. Safe Mode loads only the minimum required drivers, which can prevent the virus from protecting itself while you try to delete it.

1. Click **Start** $\rightarrow$ **Settings** $\rightarrow$ **System** $\rightarrow$ **Recovery**.
2. Under **Advanced startup**, click **Restart now**.
3. After restarting, go to: **Troubleshoot** $\rightarrow$ **Advanced options** $\rightarrow$ **Startup Settings** $\rightarrow$ **Restart**.
4. Press **5** or **F5** for **Safe Mode with Networking**.

### Step 2: Clean Up Your Browser (Adware Removal)
Adware usually lives in your browser as malicious extensions or modified search settings.

1. **Remove Suspicious Extensions:** 
   - In Chrome/Edge, go to the "Extensions" menu. Remove anything you didn’t install yourself or that looks suspicious.
2. **Reset Browser Settings:** 
   - Go to Settings $\rightarrow$ Reset Settings $\rightarrow$ Restore settings to their original defaults. This removes malicious homepages and search engines.
3. **Clear Cache/Cookies:** Clear your browsing history and cache to remove tracking cookies used by adware.

### Step 3: Uninstall Suspicious Software
1. Press `Win + R`, type `appwiz.cpl`, and press Enter.
2. Sort the list by **Date**. Look for programs installed around the time the problems started.
3. If you see a program you don't recognize (especially "Free PDF Converters," "PC Cleaners," or strange toolbars), right-click and **Uninstall**.

### Step 4: Run Specialized Scanning Tools
Standard antivirus sometimes misses "PUPs" (Potentially Unwanted Programs). Use these specific tools in this order:

1. **Malwarebytes (Free Version):** This is the industry standard for removing adware and trojans. Download it, run a "Threat Scan," and quarantine everything it finds.
2. **AdwCleaner (by Malwarebytes):** This is a tiny, free tool specifically designed to kill browser hijackers and adware. It doesn't require installation.
3. **Windows Defender Offline Scan:** 
   - Go to **Settings** $\rightarrow$ **Privacy & Security** $\rightarrow$ **Windows Security** $\rightarrow$ **Virus & threat protection**.
   - Click **Scan options** $\rightarrow$ select **Microsoft Defender Offline scan** $\rightarrow$ **Scan now**. Your computer will restart and scan before Windows even loads.

### Step 5: Clean the Startup and Registry
If you still see pop-ups, a script might be triggering them at startup.

1. **Task Manager:** Press `Ctrl + Shift + Esc`, go to the **Startup** tab, and **Disable** any unknown or suspicious apps.
2. **Check Task Scheduler:** Search for "Task Scheduler" in the Start menu. Look through the "Task Scheduler Library" for tasks with weird names (random letters/numbers) that trigger every few minutes. Delete them.

### Step 6: Final Security Hardening
Once the system is clean, prevent it from happening again:

*   **Update Everything:** Run Windows Update and update all your apps.
*   **Change Your Passwords:** If you had a "Trojan" or "Stealer," your passwords may have been compromised. Change your primary email and banking passwords **after** the PC is clean.
*   **Install an Ad-Blocker:** Install **uBlock Origin** on your browser. This stops most adware from ever reaching your machine.

---

### When to give up and "Nuke" it (Clean Install)
If you find that:
*   The virus keeps coming back after a reboot.
*   Your files are encrypted (Ransomware).
*   You cannot open the Task Manager or Registry Editor.

**The only 100% certain way to remove deep-rooted malware is a Clean Install:** Back up your photos and documents to an external drive, then use the "Reset this PC" option in Windows Settings (choosing **"Remove everything"**).
